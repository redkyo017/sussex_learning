Quantum computing technology is still in early stages of development. The concepts that we have seen so far will most likely form the basis of practical quantum computing systems, in years to come. In the meantime, we may consider alternative approaches to solving problems with quantum computers: continuous-time quantum evolution, adiabatic quantum computation in particular, Hamiltonian simulation, and Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz (QAOA). These represent more experimental techniques to solve problems with quantum computers, whose principles are sound but whose practical performance is still being explored.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

- state the connection between unitary transformations and continuous-time evolution of a quantum state under a constant Hamiltonian
- describe the basic principles of the adiabatic quantum algorithm as an alternative to classical search algorithms
- explain the basic principle of Trotterisation in simulating Hamiltonians using discrete unitary operations
- describe the structure of a QAOA procedure.

#### How to approach this week’s studies

- This week's study material is meant to provide you with some exposure to more advanced topics in quantum information processing. You are not expected to understand this material in-depth. You should aim to grasp these concepts on a high level — Hamiltonian evolution, operator norms, the adiabatic theorem, and Trotterisation — and understand how this informs the adiabatic algorithm, Hamiltonian simulation, and QAOA.

You will not be tested on this material. But the more detail you understand this material in, the more prepared you will be to handle these concepts when you next encounter them.
