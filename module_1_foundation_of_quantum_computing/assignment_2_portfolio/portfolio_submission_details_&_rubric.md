### Purpose of the Portfolio assessment
The purpose of this assessment is to check your understanding of what has been covered in Weeks 1–6.

### Learning outcomes
The learning outcomes of this assessment are:

- systematically comprehend and analyse the difference between quantum states and randomness
- analyse and interpret simple quantum processes
- valuate and assess simple communication procedures, such as quantum teleportation
- systematically recognise, comprehend and critically appraise applications of quantum computing technology.
### Details & Portfolio problems
For this assessment, you will be required to upload a PDF file below, containing solutions to the Portfolio problems given in the weekly Independent study activities in Weeks 2–6.

### Marking rubric
Portfolio problems
Please refer to 'Five steps to problem solving: How to gain marks' on the Quantum Computation Resources page for marking criteria for the weekly Portfolio problems.

Each week's Portfolio problem (or in many cases, each part of the week's Portfolio problem) has a defined grade total representing how much it is worth. These are all assessed in increments of 0.5 marks. Taken all together, a maximum of 100 marks are available.

A few select questions involve only exposition, or only mathematics. Apart from those exceptional cases, each part will be assessed for the following qualities:
