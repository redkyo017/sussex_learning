# Week 5: Quantum simulation: Introduction
## Intro
The term ‘simulation’ usually refers to emulating a process in nature, in a laboratory, on a factory production line, or on a computer, so that possible outcomes can be understood and anticipated with reduced effort or risk. When complex quantum processes are important, the capability even of today’s supercomputers is often insufficient to capture all relevant aspects of the process in question. Quantum simulators can provide a solution by mapping the problem onto a different quantum system that can be handled and observed in the laboratory. The potential implications range from loss-free powerlines to novel drug design.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

explain the limits of using classical computers to simulate quantum systems
describe the meaning of quantum simulation
explain how quantum simulators are currently implemented in research laboratory setups
describe the types of problems that arise from mapping more complex real systems into model systems.
