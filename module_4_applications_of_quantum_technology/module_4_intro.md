## What you will learn and how 

This module will give an overview of key emerging quantum technology applications with an emphasis on those areas where the transition from laboratory to industrial application spaces is more established. Imaging, sensing, timing, communication and computing in various contexts such as biomedical diagnostics, secure information exchange and navigation will be discussed. In each technology area, the specific features and performance characteristics of the novel technologies that set them apart from their incumbent counterparts will be laid out first. Selected examples will then be used to illustrate how these characteristics translate to tangible benefits in real-world applications.

Over the seven weeks, we will cover the following topics:

-   An overview of the main types of quantum technologies
-   Quantum sensors in clocks and magnetometers
-   Quantum sensors and inertial forces
-   Quantum communication
-   Quantum simulation
-   Quantum computing
-   General considerations of quantum technology

One of the most effective ways to apply and consolidate your learning of the various forms of quantum technologies is through investigating a specific area of application and the advantages of the quantum-based solution in respect to a limitation in the corresponding classical technology. Each week you will be asked to conduct this investigation for each type of quantum technology covered in the week's material.

At the end of each week, you will have a short quiz to test your understanding.

Live sessions will provide you with the opportunity to interact with the academic team, ask questions, or clarify points you covered during learning. For more information about the sessions, please [check the module calendar](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/calendar) and the Live sessions pages across the module.

## Module learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

-   demonstrate systematic understanding of key capabilities of various quantum technologies from an application point of view
-   systematically understand key advantages of general and application-specific quantum technologies over their conventional counterparts
-   demonstrate critical awareness of current development stage and maturity of quantum solutions in various fields
-   critically appraise the benefits of quantum technology for selected test cases.

## Assessment submission guidelines

### **Assessment submission**

It is your responsibility to submit your assessment in the right format and in the right place. All assessments have been set up for electronic submission and have a submission point under the ‘Assignments’ tab of this module. You will be able to use this point to submit work while it is open for submission and review feedback once it has been released by your Online Tutor.

If you are having difficulty submitting your assessment, please [refer to the e-Submission guidance for students](http://www.sussex.ac.uk/tel/submission/students) ![](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/courses/42093/files/6842277/preview?location=course_syllabus_42093) or contact your Student Success Advisor.

### **Anonymity**

The marking of assessed work should be conducted anonymously by candidate numbers rather than names as far as reasonably practicable (for some types of assessments, anonymity is impossible, such as presentations).

When submitting your work, please use **your candidate number**. Do not include your name in the title of the document, in the file name or within it.

If you are having difficulty submitting your work, please **do not** email your work to your Module Leader or Online Tutor. Instead, please contact your Student Success Advisor.

### **Late submission**

If you miss an assessment submission deadline, you can submit the assessment **within 24 hours** of the published deadline, provided your assessment allows it. If you submit **up to** **24 hours** later than the assessment deadline, your assessment will incur a penalty deduction of five percentage points (not 5% of the actual mark).

For example, if you’re awarded a mark of 70% for an assessment, but submit within the 24-hour lateness period, your overall mark will drop to 65%.

No work shall be accepted after the 24-hour penalty period: it will be classed as a non-submission and a mark of zero will be recorded against the assessment.

Late submission is available for **specific** assessments.

If you’re unsure whether late submission applies to a particular assessment on a module, please [check Sussex Direct](https://direct.sussex.ac.uk/) ![](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/courses/42093/files/6842277/preview?location=course_syllabus_42093) and navigate to Study > Timetable > Assessment Deadlines.

This will list all your module assessment types and deadlines. If the assessment has a 24-hour penalty period, you will see (+1) next to the deadline date and time. If it does not have this, the assessment **does not** have a late submission period. Alternatively, you can contact your Student Success Advisor.

Plagiarism, collusion, personation and cheating in exams are all forms of academic misconduct, which the university takes very seriously. Every year, some students commit academic misconduct unintentionally because they did not know what was expected of them. The consequences for committing academic misconduct can be severe, so it’s important you familiarise yourself with what it is and how to avoid it. The University’s regulations on academic misconduct are published in Section 2.9 of [the Examination and Assessment Regulations Handbook](http://www.sussex.ac.uk/adqe/standards/examsandassessment) ![](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/courses/42093/files/6842277/preview?location=course_syllabus_42093).

### **Marking and feedback**

Following your assessment submissions, feedback will be provided to enable you to reflect on your own performance and how you can improve in the future.

After your assessment has been completed, your work will be marked and checked. Your work will be marked by the Module Leader. **You will receive feedback within 15 working days**.

Assessments over 30% are required to be internally moderated. Moderation is completed by another academic. They determine if the marking process has been conducted appropriately, in a fair and reliable manner, and consistently in accordance with the approved marking criteria and assessment task.

An external examiner will also be used: this is an external expert appointed to assess the academic rigour of a unit on completion. In general, they will also check a handful of papers and the quality of feedback.

There will then be an internal Progression and Award Board (PAB) meeting where individual results are approved. Until this meeting has taken place, all grades are provisional. You should [check Sussex Direct](https://direct.sussex.ac.uk/) ![](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/courses/42093/files/6842277/preview?location=course_syllabus_42093) for your final marks for each module, as this is where your assessment weightings are calculated.
