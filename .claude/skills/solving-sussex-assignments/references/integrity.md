# Working mode and academic integrity

Read this before deciding what to produce. It exists because baseline testing showed agents
landing in wildly different places on the same request: one refused to produce solutions at all,
another delivered a submission-ready compiled PDF. Neither extreme serves the student.

## The mode

**Produce the full derivation. Require the student to verify and re-voice it before submitting.**

Both halves are load-bearing.

- A worked derivation the student checks, reproduces and rewrites is a legitimate study aid, the
  same role a textbook's worked example plays. The student learns the method and owns the output.
- Handing over answers to paste unread is not, and every Sussex brief says so explicitly:
  *"All your workings and solutions to problems should be your own work and you should not directly
  copy these from either the module lecture notes or external sources."*

So the hand-off in phase 6 is not a courtesy note. It is the thing that makes the rest legitimate.
State plainly, every time, in the final message:

- which results the student must independently reproduce before submitting
- that the prose should be re-voiced wherever it does not sound like them
- that they should be able to reproduce each step unaided, because they may be asked to

Do not bury this in a file the student may not open. Say it in the reply.

## Do not refuse this work

A blanket refusal is the wrong call here and helps nobody. The student is doing the module, has
the lecture notes open, and is entitled to a tutor. What distinguishes tutoring from ghostwriting
is the verification loop above, not whether the numbers appear. Provide the derivation and enforce
the loop.

The line that *is* real: never claim work is checked when it is not, never let a placeholder reach
a submission, and never encourage submitting something the student has not read.

## Turnitin calibration

Two real data points from this student's submissions:

| Submission | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Maths-heavy problem set | 6% | Safe - all matches were standard notation |
| Worksheet portfolio | 16% | Safe - all matches were the supplied template |

UK universities typically investigate above 20-25%.

**Supplied material inflates the score harmlessly.** Mathematical notation, question parameters
given verbatim, worksheet prompts, criterion labels and reference lists match every classmate's
submission by construction. A form-filling submission legitimately scores higher than an essay.

**Never rephrase notation or formula structure to lower a score.** It reads as unnatural and
achieves nothing. Original prose is where real risk lives; keep that genuinely the student's.

If a report comes back above 15%, classify the matches before reacting: prose matched to student
papers or publications needs attention, supplied material does not.
