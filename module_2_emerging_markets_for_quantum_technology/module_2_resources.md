### Typesetting in LaTeX

The mathematical content in your report and in the solutions you develop are required to be typeset to a professional standard. There are various ways to achieve this, but we recommend that by far the best method is to use LaTeX. 

This is a typesetting system that is used by almost all professional physicists and mathematicians to typeset books, papers, reports and other documents. LaTeX is very flexible, but it has a learning curve. If you have not used it before, you are strongly recommended to devote some time to getting used to its mark-up language. This should be done well before you need to write your report.   

For basic typesetting, LaTeX is much like using a word processor such as Microsoft Word. However, inclusion of figures, images, citations/references and snippets of computer code can be more difficult – although the final results are often better. With sufficient effort, you can even produce high-quality Feynman diagrams that look just like those in Peskin and Schroeder \[1\].

This is especially true for citations and preparation of your reference list, which LaTeX can do for you automatically. This is an enormous time saver. If you do encounter difficulties, most supervisors will use LaTeX to write their own research papers and will be able to assist you.

For more information read [LaTeX – A document preparation systemLinks to an external site.](https://www.latex-project.org/)

There are four options for using LaTex. Read the descriptions about all these ways of working and decide on the best approach for you: 

Option 1 (Recommended): LaTeX editor within Canvas (for tasks within the Foundations of Quantum Mechanics course)

 The simplest solution for tasks within the course is to use the LaTeX editor within Canvas to add your solutions directly, **this is the option we recommend**. There is no need to download anything onto your computer.

Option 2: LaTeX with Overleaf  This is an optional tool, not supported by the University of Sussex, which requires you to register and sign in. It is a cloud-based solution that allows you to edit your report online and collaborate with other people – this makes sharing with your supervisor very easy. Because it runs in the cloud, there is nothing to install on your personal machine. 

Overleaf is a commercial product, but it has a free tier that is sufficient for working on your report. You can access the free version by registering. You do not have to sign up to a plan.

[Visit the Overleaf website if you would like to register.Links to an external site.](https://www.overleaf.com/user/subscription/plans) 

**What is good**  

-   You do not have to install anything on your own machine.  
-   Many LaTeX extension packages are available, so you do not have to find and install them yourself.  
-   It has version history and reviewing features, which makes it easy to iterate over drafts with your supervisor.  
-   You will not lose your work because it is stored in the cloud (but you should still keep a backup).  
-   The editor has built-in spellcheck.

**What is not so good** 

-   You need to be online: it only works when you are connected to the internet.  
-   The editor is decent but is not the best.  
-   There is no built-in grammar check, but you can use browser extensions from Grammarly or LanguageTool (links are provided at the end of this section). These provide a basic free service (although of course they want you to upgrade to a premium service – but there is no need to do this). The situation is a bit confusing. Overleaf is currently (autumn 2022) in the middle of rolling out a new editor. You may have access to this, or you may not. The Grammarly plugin seems to work with the new editor, but not the old one. The LanguageTool plugin seems to be the other way round. Probably this situation will stabilise over the year.

-   **Warning: Part of the mark scheme is related to proper typesetting and layout of mathematical content.** If there are significant errors, you may have a mark deduction applied.

### Six steps to problem solving: How to gain marks [BACK TO TOP ![](https://canvas.sussex.ac.uk/courses/25919/files/3393812/preview "Back to top")](#top "Back to top") 

These are six recommended steps to problem solving to help you to gain maximum marks 

1\. Write a list of what you know (marks available for this)

-   -   variables mentioned specifically in the question 
    -   variables that have inferred values (for example, object at rest: _v_ = 0 m/s).  2\. Draw a diagram 

This is very heavily weighted in the marking because it is so important to communicate well. Do not miss out on gaining credit for the diagram – it does not need to be a work of art:  

-   -   Use a plan view (from above) or side-on view. Usually, 2D is preferable to 3D.  
    -   The neater the better. Use a ruler and keep to a simple outline drawing.  
    -   You must show dimensions, parameters, vector directions and significant values/facts.  
    -   Ensure your diagram is not too small. Diagrams must be clear and at least one-eighth of an A4 page, or larger.  3\. State the method, physical concept of law you intend to use and use relevant formulae required

Credit is given for properly showing the concepts used and giving an explanation.  

-   -   Explain the physics used. For example: using conservation of energy; resolving vector directions perpendicular and parallel to a slope; using Newton’s Second Law (_F_ \= ma), and so on.  
    -   Remember, it may be necessary to substitute one formula into another or rearrange formulae to make a different variable the subject.  
    -   If the problem requires breaking down into different parts or use of an additional concept in a later part of the solution, the approach must be stated for each part.  4\. Algebra (lots of marks given if you show the algebra)

Show the steps used to eliminate unknowns and reduce calculation involved to the minimum required. Numerical values that are known should not be inserted until the calculation has been reduced algebraically to the simplest form possible.  

-   -   This eliminates errors that are easily made using a calculator.  
    -   This speeds up identifying mistakes made throughout your working. 
    -   It also improves your marks because credit can be awarded for partial algebraic solutions that cannot be given for partial numerical answers.  5\. Substitute values: and don’t forget the units on all the values!!!

-   -   Will the calculation give the correct units for the final answer?
    -   Check the units make sense every time values are substituted.
    -   If Algebra is used effectively, it should only be necessary to do this in one single line of working but if omitted, marks available for this will not be gained.  6\. Calculate value and write the answer as a statement.

-   -   Check the final answer has the correct units, fulfils the question and has not gone off on a tangent or given the answer to something else (which is easily done when there are several parts required for a question).  
    -   Use standard form and the appropriate number of significant figures for the question.  
    -   Check the order of magnitude of the numerical part of the answer makes sense and is realistic.  

**Scientists and mathematicians are lazy people; do not make extra work for yourself. We work this way because it saves us time and effort and helps us to communicate ideas efficiently.**
