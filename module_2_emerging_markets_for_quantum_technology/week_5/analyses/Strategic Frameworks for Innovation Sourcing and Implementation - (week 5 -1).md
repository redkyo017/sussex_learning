# Strategic Frameworks for Innovation Sourcing and Implementation - (week 5 -1)

Based on the recently uploaded week 5 resources, here is a synthesis and analysis of innovation sourcing, strategic models, and Richard Rumelt's framework for strategy.

### \*\*Main Ways Firms Source Ideas for Innovation\*\*
Innovation is a "coupling process" that links technical capabilities with customer needs \[1\]. Firms do not rely on a single path but rather a "knowledge spaghetti" model where scientific discovery and market demand continuously shape one another \[2\].

\*   \*\*Knowledge Push and Need Pull:\*\* Ideas originate from either pushing the frontiers of science (e.g., R&D labs creating new materials or software) or being triggered by market demands and customer frustrations \[2-4\].
\*   \*\*Users as Active Innovators:\*\* Firms often source ideas from \*\*lead users\*\* (who identify requirements months or years before the general market) or \*\*extreme users\*\* (who operate under demanding constraints) \[5, 6\]. These users frequently modify products themselves, providing blueprints for radical design breakthroughs \[7, 8\].
\*   \*\*Recombinant Innovation and Technology Transfer:\*\* This involves applying established concepts or business models from one industry to a completely different context, which leverages pre-validated solutions and minimizes R&D risk \[7, 9\].
\*   \*\*Open Innovation:\*\* Modern firms use an "open" model where ideas flow across organizational boundaries through \*\*licensing, joint ventures, and spin-outs\*\* \[10-12\].
\*   \*\*Search Strategies and Methods:\*\* Strategic searching is a board-level responsibility that involves \*\*actively scanning for external opportunities\*\* \[13\]. Specific methods include:
    \*   \*\*Scouts:\*\* Individuals sent out to track new technological triggers \[14\].
    \*   \*\*Deep Dives:\*\* Studying what people actually do rather than what they say they do \[14\].
    \*   \*\*Online Communities:\*\* Engaging with technically informed members of digital networks \[14\].
    \*   \*\*Corporate Ventures:\*\* Using entrepreneurship to stimulate talent both inside and outside the firm \[14\].

### \*\*Critical Analysis of Strategic Approaches\*\*
The resources differentiate between the \*\*Rationalist (Planning)\*\* school and the \*\*Resource/Capabilities-based (Incrementalist)\*\* school.

#### \*\*Rationalist (Planning) Strategy\*\*
This school follows an "ends-means" logic: appraise choices, determine what to do, and then act \[15\]. It is typically top-down and focused on external market positions.
\*   \*\*Core Tools:\*\* Examples include \*\*Porter’s Five Forces\*\* (seeking market imperfections for monopoly profits) and the \*\*Boston Box\*\* (targeting high-growth markets) \[15-17\].
\*   \*\*Critical Limitations:\*\*
    \*   \*\*Underestimates Technology:\*\* It fails to account for how innovation can disrupt all five of Porter's forces (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, and buyer/supplier power) \[18, 19\].
    \*   \*\*Neglects Internal Strengths:\*\* By focusing primarily on competitors, it overlooks whether strategic objectives actually match the firm's internal capabilities \[19\].
    \*   \*\*Implementation Naivety:\*\* It assumes that the division between "strategy" and "implementation" is clear-cut and that implementation is easy, which is rarely true in complex settings \[20\].

#### \*\*Resource/Capabilities-based (Incrementalist) Strategy\*\*
Based on what firms actually do, this approach recognizes the limits of knowledge and adopts a flexible "trial and error" method \[21, 22\].
\*   \*\*Core Logic:\*\* Strategy must identify, develop, and exploit \*\*firm-specific knowledge\*\* \[23\]. It assumes technical change is \*\*path-dependent\*\*—where a firm can go is constrained by where it has been in the past \[24\].
\*   \*\*Strategic Advantage:\*\* It allows firms to adapt to "unplanned market signals," as seen in the example of \*\*Honda's success\*\* in the US motorcycle market, which resulted from learning through failure rather than perfect initial planning \[25\].
\*   \*\*Balancing Trade-offs:\*\* It requires managing the conflict between developing specialized knowledge and integrating that knowledge across the whole organization \[26, 27\].

### \*\*Differentiating Rumelt's Good and Bad Strategies\*\*
Richard Rumelt argues that the core of strategy is discovering critical factors and designing a coordinated way to deal with them \[28\].

\*   \*\*Good Strategy (The "Kernel"):\*\*
    \*   \*\*Diagnosis:\*\* Honestly defining the obstacles and challenges the firm faces \[29, 30\].
    \*   \*\*Guiding Policy:\*\* Defining a specific approach to deal with the obstacles identified in the diagnosis \[29\].
    \*   \*\*Coherent Actions:\*\* Coordinated resource allocations and actions that mutually reinforce one another \[29\].
    \*   \*\*Example:\*\* \*\*Steve Jobs\*\* at Apple in 1998 saved the company not through complex planning but by "waiting for the next big thing" (iPad, iPhone), understanding that technology comes in waves \[31, 32\].

\*   \*\*Bad Strategy:\*\*
    \*   \*\*Fluff:\*\* Using broad goals, ambitions, visions, and values (e.g., "We want to be the top firm in Europe") to mask the lack of a real plan \[28\].
    \*   \*\*Ignoring Choice:\*\* Failing to focus resources by refusing to decide "to do this and not this" \[28, 33\].
    \*   \*\*"Doorknob Polishing":\*\* Engaging in minor activities like making alliances or firmware tweaks that don't address the fundamental challenges \[31\].
    \*   \*\*The "Budget Drumbeat":\*\* Mistaking financial forecasting or 5-year budget plans for actual strategy \[33, 34\].
