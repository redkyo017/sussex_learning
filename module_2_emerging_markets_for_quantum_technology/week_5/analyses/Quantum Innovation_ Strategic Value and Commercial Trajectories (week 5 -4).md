# Quantum Innovation: Strategic Value and Commercial Trajectories (week 5 -4)

Based on the sources provided, quantum technology (QT) firms operate within a \*\*science-based\*\* innovation trajectory, where business models must account for high technical uncertainty, long development cycles, and the critical need for \*\*complementary assets\*\* to capture value.

### 1. Quantum Technology Business Model Matrix

| Feature | Value Creation (Customer Willingness to Pay) | Value Capture (Covering Costs/Profit) | Financial Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Quantum Computing\*\* | Solving complex, intractable problems in \*\*optimization, materials simulation, and drug discovery\*\* \[1-3\]. | Massive \*\*patent portfolios\*\* (e.g., IBM’s 1,000+ patents) and shifting to a \*\*"quantum-as-a-service"\*\* cloud model \[4-6\]. | Transitioning from \*\*heavy government/state subsidy\*\* and VC funding to potential commercial revenue as "fault-tolerance" is achieved \[7-9\]. |

### 2. Main Sources of Innovation
The primary source of innovation for QT firms is \*\*knowledge push\*\* originating from basic and applied research in corporate R&D and universities \[24, 25\]. Because QT involves the creation of new physical "effects" (e.g., entanglement), innovation is initiated by \*\*design and experimentation\*\* rather than just pure theory \[26-28\].

\*   \*\*Why:\*\* Technical change in this sector is \*\*cumulative and path-dependent\*\* \[29, 30\]. Firms must engage in research as an \*\*"admission ticket"\*\* to global scientific networks to recognize and assimilate external breakthroughs—a capability known as \*\*absorptive capacity\*\* \[31, 32\].

### 3. Differences by Sector and Customer Needs
Innovation sources differ based on the functional requirements of the end-user:
\*   \*\*Sensors (Construction/Mining):\*\* Sourced from \*\*"reality checks" and trials in harsh environments\*\*, where the guidance of civil engineers ensures the technology survives site-specific conditions \[33, 34\].
\*   \*\*Computing (Pharma/Logistics):\*\* Sourced from \*\*computational benchmarks\*\* where quantum speed-up is tested against specific "Grand Challenges" like molecular dynamics \[35-37\].
\*   \*\*Communication (Defense/Finance):\*\* Sourced from the need for \*\*unconditional security\*\*, focusing on the development of "quantum-safe" protocols and standardisation \[38-40\].

### 4. Comparison with Pharmaceuticals and Retailing
\*   \*\*QT vs. Pharmaceuticals:\*\* Both are science-based and rely heavily on \*\*patents\*\* and links to universities \[41, 42\]. However, QT often lacks the highly predictive scientific models found in some areas of biology, necessitating a more \*\*experimental "cut and try"\*\* approach to hardware design \[43, 44\].
\*   \*\*QT vs. Retailing:\*\* Retailing is \*\*information-intensive\*\* or \*\*design-intensive\*\* \[25, 41\]. While a retailer like Zara sources innovation from real-time market signals (need-pull), a QT firm often creates its own market by developing a technology that does what was previously thought impossible \[45-47\]. In retailing, R&D is often irrelevant; in QT, it is the bedrock \[48, 49\].

### 5. Relative Importance of Innovation Sources
1.  \*\*Internal R&D:\*\* Vital for building \*\*absorptive capacity\*\* to understand external science \[31, 32\].
2.  \*\*University Links:\*\* Critical as \*\*knowledge mediators\*\*; most QT start-ups (e.g., QuantumCTek, Origin Quantum) are university spin-offs \[50, 51\].
3.  \*\*Customers (Lead Users):\*\* Highly important for finding "best-use" commercial cases, which are currently unknown \[52, 53\].
4.  \*\*Suppliers:\*\* Provide essential \*\*supporting technologies\*\* (cryogenics, lasers, vacuum systems) that often limit the performance of quantum devices \[54-56\].

### 6. Non-Financial Value Capture in Large Firms (Google, IBM)
Large departments use non-financial strategies to secure long-term leadership:
\*   \*\*Reputation and Branding:\*\* Acts as a \*\*cospecialized asset\*\*; developers are more likely to write software for an IBM computer because they trust the brand's commitment to the platform \[57-59\].
\*   \*\*Ecosystem Orchestration:\*\* By creating \*\*open architectures\*\* and global university programs, firms like IBM induce third parties to create "co-specialized" software, effectively locking users into their ecosystem \[57, 60, 61\].
\*   \*\*Monitoring and Evaluation:\*\* In-house research groups act as a \*\*radar\*\* to monitor and evaluate research conducted elsewhere, preventing the firm from being "blindsided" by a competitor's breakthrough \[32, 62\].
\*   \*\*Standard Setting:\*\* Leading global committees on \*\*standardization\*\* allows these firms to ensure that future industry norms align with their own technical trajectories \[19-21\].
