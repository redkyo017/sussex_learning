# IBM’s Quantum Strategy: Rationalist Planning and Dynamic Capabilities - (Week 5 -2)

To help you master this task, here is a synthesized analysis of a leading quantum firm—\*\*IBM\*\*—evaluating its strategy through the lenses of rationalist planning and dynamic capabilities.

### \*\*1. Selected Firm and Strategy: IBM\*\*
IBM follows a \*\*"science-push" strategy\*\* coupled with a shift toward becoming a \*\*quantum services provider\*\* \[1, 2\]. Their strategy involves solving "Grand Challenges" through high-investment R&D, such as their promise to achieve a \*\*1000-qubit quantum computer\*\* \[3, 4\]. Crucially, IBM leverages its \*\*reputation and brand\*\* to establish PC-like open architectures, inducing third-party developers to create software that is partly \*\*co-specialized\*\* with IBM hardware \[5, 6\].

### \*\*2. Rationalist vs. Dynamic Capabilities Frameworks\*\*

#### \*\*Rationalist Approach (Porter’s Five Forces)\*\*
A rationalist analysis of IBM’s position focuses on external market imperfections to secure monopoly profits \[7\].
\*   \*\*Rivalry:\*\* Intense competition exists between IBM, Google, and state-backed Chinese firms like Origin Quantum \[4, 8\]. 
\*   \*\*Threat of New Entrants:\*\* While capital requirements are massive, national strategies (like the UK’s £2.5 billion commitment) subsidize new entrants, potentially disrupting IBM’s position \[9-11\].
\*   \*\*Threat of Substitutes:\*\* Classical supercomputers currently remain a viable substitute for NISQ (noisy intermediate-scale quantum) devices until \*\*fault-tolerant quantum computing\*\* is achieved \[12, 13\].
\*   \*\*Evaluation:\*\* The rationalist school often \*\*underestimates the power of technology\*\* to disrupt these forces and overestimates how much choice managers have over their technical trajectory \[14, 15\].

#### \*\*Dynamic Capabilities (Business Model Framework)\*\*
This school focuses on how IBM exploits its \*\*firm-specific knowledge\*\* and handles uncertainty through learning \[16, 17\].
\*   \*\*Path Dependency:\*\* IBM’s strategy is \*\*path-dependent\*\*, leveraging wealth and know-how gained from the previous computing and internet revolutions \[18, 19\].
\*   \*\*Absorptive Capacity:\*\* IBM invests in basic research as an \*\*"admission ticket"\*\* to global scientific networks, allowing them to recognize and assimilate external discoveries \[20, 21\].
\*   \*\*Evaluation:\*\* This framework better explains IBM’s success, as it recognizes that technical change is a \*\*step-by-step process of trial and error\*\* rather than a clean, linear plan \[22, 23\].

### \*\*3. Value Creation and Capture Over Time\*\*
\*   \*\*Value Creation:\*\* IBM creates value by developing hardware and \*\*platform-agnostic operating systems\*\* that aim to solve problems currently intractable for classical computers \[24, 25\].
\*   \*\*Value Capture:\*\* IBM prevents value leakage to imitators through a massive portfolio of \*\*1,000+ patents\*\* \[26, 27\]. They also use \*\*lead-time advantages\*\* and \*\*global marketing networks\*\* to reach customers before competitors can react \[28, 29\].
\*   \*\*Temporal Changes:\*\* In the current \*\*preparadigmatic stage\*\*, value is created through design competition \[30, 31\]. As the industry moves toward a \*\*dominant design\*\*, IBM’s strategy must shift from radical product innovation to \*\*process innovation\*\* and low-cost manufacturing \[32, 33\].

### \*\*4. Industry Domination in Five Years\*\*
In five years, the industry is likely to be dominated by firms that effectively manage their \*\*complementary assets\*\* \[34, 35\]. 
\*   \*\*IBM and Google\*\* are expected to dominate the \*\*quantum computing\*\* sector due to their large-scale hardware roadmaps and established developer ecosystems \[8, 36\].
\*   \*\*Toshiba\*\* is positioned to lead \*\*quantum communication\*\* (QKD) due to its demonstration of record-breaking photon counting and leadership in global standards-setting groups \[37, 38\].
\*   \*\*State-led Chinese firms\*\* like \*\*QuantumCTek\*\* may dominate their domestic markets and specific subsectors like satellite-based communication, provided they can overcome current geopolitical isolation from Western supply chains \[39-41\].

### \*\*Critique of Strategy Effectiveness\*\*
IBM's strategy is highly effective because it acknowledges that \*\*"theory is a weak guide to practice"\*\* \[42\]. By building a "quantum-ready" ecosystem through the \*\*Hartree National Centre\*\* and its Global University Programs, IBM ensures it has the \*\*absorptive capacity\*\* to pivot as new quantum phenomena are discovered \[43, 44\]. However, their success remains vulnerable to the \*\*"false summit" effect\*\*, where solving one technical barrier (like qubit scaling) reveals hidden new problems that can delay commercial returns for decades \[45, 46\].
