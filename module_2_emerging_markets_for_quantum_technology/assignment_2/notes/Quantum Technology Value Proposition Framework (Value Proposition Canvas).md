# \*\*Value Proposition (The Value Map)\*\*

### \*\*Products and Features\*\*
\*   \*\*What can help a customer get a job done?\*\*
    Quantum value propositions typically deliver a combination of physical hardware, remote access portals, and customized algorithm libraries \[4\]. This includes \*\*Cloud-accessible quantum processors (QPUs)\*\*, hardware-optimized quantum compilers, and hybrid classical-quantum software frameworks \[4, 5\].
\*   \*\*Does it address specific jobs or a variety?\*\*
    In the current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era, products generally target \*\*highly specific mathematical bottlenecks\*\* (e.g., molecular dynamics, financial portfolio optimization, or integer factoring) rather than general-purpose computing \[6-9\]. 
\*   \*\*Are the features tangible or digital/virtual?\*\*
    Because physical quantum hardware is extremely capital-intensive and fragile to maintain, the dominant delivery channel is digital/virtual \[10, 11\]. Features are packaged as \*\*Quantum-as-a-Service (QaaS) APIs\*\*, allowing users to remotely access physical QPUs through existing classical cloud infrastructures \[5, 11\]. However, hardware-centric models (such as photonic accelerators) do offer tangible, room-temperature systems deployable directly in standard high-performance computing (HPC) server racks \[12\].

### \*\*Gain Creators\*\*
\*   \*\*How do features create savings in time, money, and effort?\*\*
    Gain creators show how quantum architectures can compress discovery and computation timelines \[5\]. For example, in drug discovery, they can map molecular structures and amino acid combinations in days rather than years, radically reducing R&D costs \[5, 13\].
\*   \*\*Do they produce expected outcomes or exceed expectations?\*\*
    They produce expected outcomes such as quadratic or exponential computational speedups, \*\*absolute cryptographic security\*\*, and a dramatic reduction in physical prototyping costs \[14\]. In logistics, they offer 30% faster route-planning and 20% improvements in portfolio optimization \[15\].
\*   \*\*How do they outperform existing solutions?\*\*
    They outperform classical supercomputers by solving high-dimensional combinatorial problems that scale exponentially and would otherwise take millions of years to run \[14, 16\]. Quantum-inspired algorithms (such as Matrix Product States) can also outperform existing classical systems by significantly compressing data storage and accelerating neural network training \[17, 18\].

### \*\*Pain Relievers\*\*
\*   \*\*How do features prevent frustrations and annoyances?\*\*
    Pain relievers reduce the frustration of long queue runtimes and massive classical compute bottlenecks \[5, 14\]. They streamline complex hardware instruction sets and automate qubit control so that software designers do not need to deal with underlying physical "strangeness" \[5, 19\].
\*   \*\*How do they improve upon under-performing solutions?\*\*
    They mitigate physical hardware noise, error rates, and rapid decoherence \[5\]. Advanced compilers and error mitigation/suppression algorithms allow noisy intermediate-scale hardware to execute complex calculations reliably \[5, 20\].
\*   \*\*Do they mitigate risks or eliminate obstacles?\*\*
    They eliminate strategic lock-in and transition risks \[21\]. In cybersecurity, they mitigate the terminal risk of future quantum decryption by introducing \*\*Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)\*\* standards and \*\*Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)\*\* hardware \[22, 23\]. Adopting these early also serves as \*\*"competitive insurance"\*\* to de-risk future disruption \[8, 24\].

# \*\*Customer Segment (The Customer Profile)\*\*

### \*\*Jobs\*\*
\*   \*\*How does your customer interact with your business?\*\*
    In deep-tech and quantum B2B markets, relationships are highly collaborative and consultative rather than transactional \[11, 25\]. Customers engage in \*\*co-discovery workshops\*\* and pilot projects to identify which of their high-value internal bottlenecks can be mapped onto quantum-accessible formulations \[11, 26\].
\*   \*\*Do their activities and goals change in different contexts?\*\*
    Yes, but their jobs are almost exclusively functional in nature \[27, 28\]. In the lab context, an aerospace engineer's goal is to solve high-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs) for fluid dynamics \[28\]. In a corporate context, a biopharma research director seeks to map molecular electron structures to discover new battery chemistries or pharmaceuticals \[5\].
\*   \*\*What products or services are they currently using to complete these activities?\*\*
    Customers currently rely on classical high-performance computing (HPC) supercomputers \[29-31\], but are forced to use \*\*severe, highly restrictive mathematical approximations\*\* that compromise the accuracy of their models \[14\].

### \*\*Gains\*\*
\*   \*\*How do customers measure performance, quality, and success?\*\*
    Enterprise buyers measure success through performance metrics like simulation fidelity, algorithmic accuracy, and the overall reduction of computing memory and time \[14, 29, 32\].
\*   \*\*Would savings in time, money, or effort be of the most value?\*\*
    \*\*Time savings\*\* represent the most urgent value \[33\]. Compressing chemical screening or financial risk assessments from weeks to minutes allows firms to capture first-mover market advantages \[13, 33\].
\*   \*\*What positive social consequences do they desire?\*\*
    Industrial customers increasingly seek positive environmental and societal impacts aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) \[34, 35\]. This includes reducing the massive carbon footprint of classical data centers (SDG 12 and 13), discovering clean energy catalysts, or developing fuel-efficient aircraft engines \[35-38\].

### \*\*Pains\*\*
\*   \*\*What are the barriers to adoption of current solutions to the problem?\*\*
    The main barriers are the immense energy consumption, exponential scaling bottlenecks, and computational latency of classical supercomputers \[14\]. For instance, classical models struggle to accurately simulate electron-level physical behaviors at the atomic level \[31\].
\*   \*\*What are the main challenges they face when interacting with current solutions?\*\*
    Running complex simulations on classical infrastructure requires extremely long compute cycles, leading to delayed decision-making and high operational costs \[14\].
\*   \*\*How are current value propositions underperforming?\*\*
    Existing classical computing value propositions are fundamentally limited by physical hardware scaling limits (e.g., Moore's Law), meaning they can no longer keep pace with the growing dimensional complexity of modern big data, molecular physics, and global logistics \[6, 14, 39\].
