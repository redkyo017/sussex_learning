# Quantum Computing Business Model Canvas (Business Model Canvas)

### \*\*Key Partners\*\*
\*   \*\*What suppliers do you need?\*\* 
    You need highly specialized component manufacturers supplying lasers, cryogenic dilution refrigerators, high-vacuum equipment, low-noise microwave amplifiers, silicon waveguides, synthetic diamond, superconductors, and single-photon detectors \[1-5\]. In particular, suppliers of helium-3 are critical to support the ultra-low temperatures required by superconducting QPUs \[6, 7\].
\*   \*\*What resources do they provide?\*\* 
    They provide physical raw materials, advanced components, and specialized fabrication/characterization tools \[8-10\]. This includes standardized silicon-on-insulator or semiconductor qubit devices \[11\], and advanced surface characterization facilities to resolve material defects \[9\].
\*   \*\*What activities do they fulfill?\*\* 
    They perform outsourced chip fabrication (such as semiconductor cleanrooms or the MESA facility) \[12, 13\], standard hardware testing, and high-level material characterization \[9, 14, 15\].
\*   \*\*Who else do you need to work with?\*\* 
    You must work with classical cloud infrastructure providers (e.g., AWS, Azure) to deliver computing resources via established channels \[16, 17\]. You also need to coordinate with academic laboratories, university-based technology transfer offices (TTOs), and university incubators to license spin-out IP and source advanced scientific talent \[18-21\].
\*   \*\*Who are the other key stakeholders?\*\* 
    Key stakeholders include national funding agencies (e.g., DARPA, IARPA, NSF, UKRI/EPSRC) that finance pre-competitive R&D \[22-25\], and national metrology or standardizing organizations (such as NIST, NPL, BSI, and ETSI) that define the technical benchmarks and cryptographic standards required for commercial deployment \[26-30\].

### \*\*Key Activities\*\*
\*   \*\*What does your value proposition require?\*\* 
    It requires high-intensity basic and applied R&D, algorithm design, software compiler engineering, hardware/software co-design, and quantum error correction (QEC) integration \[16, 17, 31-33\]. It also demands active mapping of industrial bottlenecks to create "algorithmic primitives" (such as high-performance machine learning optimizers) \[34-36\].
\*   \*\*What do your distribution channels require?\*\* 
    Channels require robust cloud platform management, maintaining low-latency APIs, and orchestrating hybrid classical-quantum cloud services (QCS) so that physical quantum hardware integrates seamlessly with classical high-performance computing (HPC) environments \[16, 17, 37, 38\].
\*   \*\*What do your customer relationships require?\*\* 
    They require highly consultative, long-term advisory activities, including "co-discovery" workshops to help clients identify high-value use cases, strategic "quantum readiness" auditing, and technical upskilling \[39-42\]. It also requires maintaining Responsible Research and Innovation (RRI) frameworks to address societal risks like data security and algorithmic bias \[43, 44\].

---

### \*\*Value Proposition\*\*
\*   \*\*What problem are you solving?\*\* 
    You are solving the exponential scaling bottleneck and extreme computational latency that classical supercomputers face when trying to solve high-dimensional combinatorial problems (e.g., molecular dynamics, financial portfolio optimization, and complex logistics) \[45-48\].
\*   \*\*What do your customers need?\*\* 
    Customers need high-fidelity simulations that bypass the severe mathematical approximations of classical systems, accelerated R&D discovery timelines, and information-theoretic security to safeguard public-key infrastructure from future decryption by quantum systems \[44, 47-49\].
\*   \*\*What do only they want?\*\* 
    They want \*\*"competitive insurance"\*\*—the ability to proactively build internal quantum readiness and upskilling programs to protect their business models and avoid strategic disruption as the industry matures \[39, 41, 50, 51\]. They also want hybrid configurations that run complex mathematical bottlenecks on QPUs while keeping the rest of the workflow on standard classical architectures to maximize near-term utility \[37, 39, 52\].

---

### \*\*Relationships\*\*
\*   \*\*What kind of relationship do they expect?\*\* 
    Because of significant client knowledge gaps, enterprise buyers expect highly consultative, collaborative, and advisory-led joint R&D relationships rather than standard transactional sales \[37-39, 42\].
\*   \*\*Are they one-off or repeat customers?\*\* 
    They are repeat customers. They buy through tiered, recurring Cloud Quantum-as-a-Service (QaaS) or Quantum Cloud Services (QCS) subscription models, long-term advisory/consulting retainers, or multi-year government research and defense contracts \[16, 37, 53\].
\*   \*\*How will you maintain the relationship?\*\* 
    Relationships are maintained through dedicated technical account managers, multi-tier Service Level Agreements (SLAs), academic-industrial developer networks (e.g., SparQ Discovery Programs), and hands-on hackathons or workshops \[37, 53-55\].

---

### \*\*Customer Segments\*\*
\*   \*\*Who are you creating value for?\*\* 
    You are creating value for advanced enterprise buyers in mathematically intensive verticals, notably Banking & Financial Services (BFSI), biopharmaceuticals, aerospace engineering, smart materials (e.g., EV battery design), logistics, and telecommunications/cybersecurity \[34, 45, 56, 57\].
\*   \*\*Who are your most important customers?\*\* 
    Your most important early customers are national governments, intelligence agencies, and defense departments (e.g., MoD, DoD, USCG) who act as "intelligent first customers" by issuing high-value procurement contracts and de-risking early technology development \[58-60\].
\*   \*\*Who else benefits from your business?\*\* 
    Society at large benefits through long-term alignment with UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), such as using quantum-designed smart materials and catalysts to combat climate change, reducing the massive carbon and energy footprint of classical data centers, and discovering personalized medical treatments \[40, 61-64\].

### \*\*Key Resources\*\*
\*   \*\*What does your value proposition require?\*\* 
    It requires deep intellectual property (IP) assets (e.g., massive patent portfolios like IBM’s 1,000+ patents to secure a legal monopoly) \[65-67\], proprietary compiler code, and specialized hardware architectures (such as superconducting, trapped-ion, or photonic processors) \[37, 45, 53\].
\*   \*\*What do your distribution channels require?\*\* 
    They require secure APIs, cloud access platform software, and access to advanced national testing and characterization laboratories (e.g., the Quantum Metrology Institute or open computational testbeds) \[14, 15, 68, 69\].
\*   \*\*What do your customer relationships require?\*\* 
    They require \*\*"quantum translators"\*\*—rare, highly interdisciplinary professionals who can bridge the gap between academic quantum physics and practical commercial application—alongside classical systems engineers upskilled in quantum literacy \[39, 70, 71\]. They also require substantial capital reserves (such as venture capital or state contracts) to survive the prolonged, pre-revenue "valley of death" \[22, 61, 62, 72\].

---

### \*\*Channels\*\*
\*   \*\*How will you reach them?\*\* 
    You reach them through online open-source machine learning integration platforms (e.g., PennyLane), developer web portals, collaborative academic networks, and direct enterprise sales teams targeting B2B/B2G buyers \[13, 17, 23\].
\*   \*\*Which channels do they use?\*\* 
    Enterprise customers primarily use cloud-based \*\*Quantum-as-a-Service (QaaS) APIs\*\* to access hardware remotely, avoiding the prohibitive cost and operational burden of managing physical QPUs in-house \[37, 56, 61\].
\*   \*\*How do they all fit together?\*\* 
    Open-source software libraries and developer workshops build early community adoption \[13, 17\]; this leads to joint custom use-case consulting engagements, which ultimately funnel enterprise users into long-term cloud-based API subscriptions hosted on major public cloud platforms \[37, 38, 56\].

### \*\*Cost Structure\*\*
\*   \*\*What costs are essential?\*\* 
    Essential costs include specialized, PhD-level human capital, cloud service operational fees, legal/regulatory ITAR compliance, and IP/patent filing fees \[37, 53, 56, 73\].
\*   \*\*Which resources are most expensive?\*\* 
    The most expensive resources are the asset-heavy cleanrooms, microfabrication facilities, vacuum systems, cryogenics, and the continuous procurement of specialized physical components \[37, 46, 53, 74\].
\*   \*\*Which activities are most expensive?\*\* 
    Physical hardware R&D, cleanroom chip fabrication, compiler/software R&D, and custom use-case consulting are the most capital-intensive activities \[17, 37, 53\]. For asset-heavy hardware developers, the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) can absorb over half of total revenue, while total operational costs can reach as high as 445.7% of revenues during early scaling \[53\].

---

### \*\*Revenue Streams\*\*
\*   \*\*What are your customers willing to pay?\*\* 
    They are willing to pay for "competitive insurance" through fixed-fee strategic readiness audits, milestone-based Proof-of-Concept (PoC) custom algorithm builds, and tiered subscription access to hardware platforms (QCS) \[37, 40, 56\].
\*   \*\*How would they prefer to pay?\*\* 
    They prefer to pay via recurring, usage-based cloud QaaS subscription models \[37, 38, 56\] or, in security-sensitive government and defense sectors, through direct, multi-year R&D and physical hardware deployment contracts \[37, 53\].
\*   \*\*How much revenue will each stream contribute?\*\* 
    While QaaS and QCS represent the dominant long-term scaling engine, near-term revenue is heavily bolstered by professional consulting services (yielding over 60% gross margins) \[73\] and passive IP licensing/royalties, which boast gross margins exceeding 90% and contribute millions of dollars in passive annual revenue \[17, 73\].
