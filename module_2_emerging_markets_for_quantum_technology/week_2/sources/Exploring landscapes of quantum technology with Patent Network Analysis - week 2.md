1. Introduction

## Quantum technology

The quantum of an object is the smallest unit (if there exists) composing it. Normally, a quantum is so small, say, smaller than 1 nanometer, that we cannot manipulate it or predict its motion by Newton's laws . Quantum theory is currently the only theory that can explain and predict the behaviour of such a tiny object. Since we human beings live in a large-scale world, there are many phenomena in quantum theory, dubbed as quantum phenomena , that are counterintuitive to our daily life. The famous ‘Schrödinger's cat’ is one of the instances (Schrödinger1935). If some of these counterintuitive quantum phenomena have applications in our technological advancement, we call such integrations between quantum theory and technology as quantum technology .

The revolution of quantum technology can be divided into two stages (Jaeger2018). Starting from the early of 1900, the first revolution of quantum technology appeared most in semiconductor materials, as they are stable and easier to manipulate and manufacture. Today, we benefit from many applications in the first revolution, such as laser (The Nobel Prize in Physics 1964), the photoelectric effect (The Nobel Prize in Physics 1921) and electrical signals. Also, nuclear magnetic resonance (The Nobel Prize in Physics 1952) enables us to create medical devices such as magnetic resonance imaging. The display devices based on quantum dots can produce pure monochromatic colour (Wood and Bulović2010).

The second revolution is based on the unusual quantum phenomena (Jaeger2018), such as quantum entanglement (Horodecki et al.2009), quantum coherence (Streltsov et al.2017), superposition (Nielsen et al.2010) , and these phenomena are hard to observe in semiconductor materials with the usual environment (e.g. the temperature or pressure). The applications of the second revolution are still under exploration and development in different fields of quantum technologies. The main categories of the quantum technologies include ‘quantum computation’, ‘quantum communication’, ‘quantum simulation’, ‘quantum sensing’, and ‘quantum theory’ (Acín et al.2018).

Note that the physical principles behind the quantum phenomena of the two quantum revolution are different. Therefore, companies or institutions with advantages in the first quantum revolution do not necessarily imply they also have advantages in the second revolution.

Quantum technologies are bringing fundamental changes to our society. In commerce, the quantum annealing algorithm is used to solve budget pricing in auctions, phylogenetics, satellite scheduling, etc., problems (McGeoch et al.2019). Quantum cryptography is used in some high-security need institutions, such as banks, governments (Riedel et al.2017). Post-quantum cryptography standardisation brings the security and interoperability on communications protocols between classic and quantum computers (Beullens et al.2021; Moody et al.2020). Due to the advantages of calculating particle dynamics, quantum simulation and quantum computing are expected to accelerate the development of new drugs (Popkin2019). Generally, quantum technologies are changing the infrastructural settings gradually in different aspects and thus lead to systematic transition in the following decades.

Despite the optimism of quantum applications, the developing process is facing several challenges on both scientific & technological and social sides. On the scientific & technological side, scalability, controllability and applicability are the issues. In labs, scientists could control quantum machines under 20 qubits (Acín et al.2018). However, the key milestone toward a commercial quantum computer is a machine that can successfully operate around 100 logic qubits simultaneously (Acín et al.2018). Also, although a superconducting circuit has advantages of integrating the wide types of qubit-based devices, the circuit is limited to its fabrication production and device parameter adjustment. The ‘quantum supremacy’ only exists if there are killer applications that operate better than classic computers, such as simulating molecular dynamics (Shen and Yang2018). Solutions from various disciplines, such as engineers, data scientists, etc., matter to these challenges.

On the social side, talents and networking activities are critical to build the quantum ecosystem (Kagermann et al.2020). Currently, numerous projects are to enhance these critical elements, such as Quantum Flagship1of European Commission (Mlynek2021), a national quantum initiative2(Monroe et al.2019) of the USA, the 13th five-year plan of China (People's Republic of China2016–2020) or the Global University Programs3from global companies. Generally, these projects aim to create a quantum ecosystem, which increases resource engagement in science and technologies (Zhang et al.2019), the encouragement to the industry involvement (Knight and Walmsley2019), and cultivation of talents and the curiosity culture (National Science and Technology Council (U.S.)2018).

In summary, the success of quantum technologies relies on the contributions between social and scientific & technological sides, and the quantum ecosystem is critical to push the movement of quantum technologies.

2. Research gap

Some research gaps remain in current studies. While policymakers acknowledge the importance of a quantum ecosystem, articles seldom presented its contents, such as the interconnectedness relations between sub-fields and elements simultaneously. Also, since the progress of quantum technologies is a long-term race, understanding the aggregations in the past time is important to resource allocations and blueprint making.

Thus, our research questions conclude as follows: How does the quantum ecosystem look like? Specifically, how the subfields of quantum technologies and the elements interconnect? Last, what elements in the ecosystem we should notice for long-term plans?

3. Theoretical background

## Innovation ecosystem and multi-level perspective

We apply the theories of the innovation ecosystem and the social-technical system & multi-level perspective framework to answer the research questions. While the innovation ecosystem describes the necessary elements for the innovation activities (Granstrand and Holgersson2020), the social-technical system emphasizes the long-term co-evolutionary progress between technological and social sides in a multi-level perspective framework (Geels2004; Markard and Truffer2008). Generally, the innovation ecosystem tells us the basic components to create similar innovative activities continually. The social-technical system tells us how those innovative activities are becoming popular and then change our society transformatively. In practice, we can find numerous cases, such as the Internet, computers, to be illustrated by the two theories. Thus, we believe it is appropriate to apply the two theories to the quantum technology case.

The common thread of the social-technical system and innovation ecosystem relies on similar elements, including the actor (such as university or company), the artefact (such as technology), and interconnectedness (such as the institution or co-created value activities). The artefact, especially the technology, could act as a driving force to the innovation activities and the linkages to connect various actors in the ecosystem (Amitrano et al.2018).

In some studies, an innovation ecosystem also refers to the social-technical system (Nykänen2017) or relevant to the social-technical system design (Pasmore et al.2019; Baxter and Sommerville2011). The actor acts as a receiver of social awareness in the environment. In the meanwhile, the actor also acts as the moderator in the production side to respond the messages from the enviornment (Walrave et al.2018). Since an actor acts as the information channel between the social and production sides, the actor is at the front line to feel the transition activities in society. The actor is also the essential unit to participate in the evolutionary events in the ecosystem (Granstrand and Holgersson2020).

Scholars had broadly used the social-technical system theory to analyze the profound transition activities in the society in a multi-level perspective framework (Geels2005; Müller and Blanquart2018). From top to bottom, the multi-level perspective framework is a three-layer analytical framework, including social-technical landscape, social-technical regime and technological niche level. The social-technical landscape stands for exogenous contents in the environment, such as the public awareness of quantum supremacy. The social-technical regime represents groups of opinion leaders in the society, who usually have a decisive influence on the rule-making activities, such as government, scientist, industrial experts. The technological group in the social-technical regime level represents the actors who dominate the mainstream technologies. Technologies in the technological niche level represent those under exploring or not well-accepted by current society. At the technological niche level, a technological trajectory converges from relative technological paradigms with continual improvements from technical regimes, such as the engineers in similar industries (Dosi1982). A technological trajectory represents a knowledge accumulation path with updated advancements from engineers in the long run. The technologies in the technological niche level may level up to be the mainstream technology in the social-technical regime level for many possibilities, such as the rising of unexpected infectious diseases in the landscape level, a breakthrough technology in the niche level, policy (lobbying) in the regime level (Müller and Blanquart2018; Edmondson, Kern, and Rogge2019).

Despite the similarities between the social-technical system and innovation ecosystems, one major notion is that the social-technical system emphasizes long-term and sustainable properties (Geels2019). The second major difference is that the key driving forces of innovations. While the innovation ecosystem emphasizes the strength of elements, especially actors, technologies and their interconnectedness, the social-technical system emphasizes the forces in multi-levels and their mutual effects to change the society transformatively. The personal hygiene case described such complex relations and mutual effects (Geels2005).

These differences lead to preferred methods and relavant critics. While social-technical system studies usually used multiple cases in a wide range of period to interpret such long-lasting transition, innovation ecosystem studies usually adopted empirical analysis and figures in a relatively short term. The former studies heavily rely on the insights of experienced scholars, and thus it has been requested to add more empirical analysis for a long time (Geels2019). Although the latter studies provide objective figures, sometimes the analytical results did not fit the understandings from the practical side. For instance, China has many patents of the 5G technologies, but it does not guarantee that China is the leader in the international infrastructural setting activities (Noble, Mutimear, and Vary2019).

In short, both theories provide insights into the innovation activities, and we may learn a better and complete story of innovation activities if we take advantages of both theories.

4. Research method

We apply network analysis on patent data from the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) to answer research questions. We utilise granted patents in the USPTO to investigate quantum technologies due to several reasons. First, the patent is a common source of innovation studies in academia. Second, a granted patent is a technical document and an instrument to protect the potential markets in the future. The United States is a competitive market and a main driving force of quantum technology. Third, since applying a patent requires much cost, the continual patent publications reflect strong commitments and expectation on values to the future. Fourth, it is less complicated to construct a well-linked patent network structure as an ecosystem in a nation scope.

In views of the social-technical system, we propose that the granted patents reflect the entire system for two reasons. First, the motivations to apply for patents are complicated and highly affected by the environment. The motivations may include market protection, policy encouragement, creating standard-like architecture, technological progress, catch-up strategy, etc., and these motivations seldom exist independently (Blind, Pohlisch, and Zi2018). Second, a granted patent is like a qualified product that wins recognitions from representative actors, such as engineers from the technical side, examinators from the government, and assignees (companies) from the market side, etc. These attributes allow the granted patent to be one representative data to reflect the social-technical system.

We focus on the network analysis method instead of quantity indicators, such as the number of citations, number of inventors, or number of forward citations,etc. The network analysis has its advantage on the feature investigation in many nodes with complex connections (De Nooy, Mrvar, and Batagelj2018). We rely on positions and connections in the network structure to find significant patents or interested actors, such as the alliance-like structure in the network. The network method has been used to investigate various ecosystems, such as knowledge and business ecosystems (Clarysse et al.2014), food, pharmacy ecosystem, etc. (Ashton2008). In this study, we rely on patent citation relations to construct the quantum ecosystem.

To obtain the initial and most general patents of quantum technologies, we queried ‘quantum’ in the Google BigQuery database4from 2000 to June 2020. As a result, we searched 1,531 patents which top terms include ‘quantum’ in its patent description. Then, we expanded relevant patents by their forward and backward citations in the PatentsView database (Baker2019). Finally, a weakly connected citation network with 8,343 patents is created and mapped by the VOSviewer5(van Eck and Waltman2010).

We focus on the two analytical units, technology and actors, in the innovation ecosystem (Granstrand and Holgersson2020). Then, we investigate the technological trajectory and focal actors as the outcome of the long-term co-evolutionary process of quantum technologies.

In the technology element, we first reveal 35 innovation topics of the quantum innovation via the community detection algorithm - Louvain Method (Multi-level coarsening + Multi-level refinement), which is based on the measurement of the link density in a subnetwork in the Pajek software6(De Nooy, Mrvar, and Batagelj2018). Then, we rely on the modularity score to measure the fitness of cluster detection. The score ranges from 0 to 1. Usually, it would be a good clustering result if the modularity score is above 0.3 (Clauset, Newman, and Moore2004). In this paper, we finalise the result of the cluster detection by relative high modularity scores, and the modularity score of cluster detection is 0.90.

We identified the technological trajectory by the main path analysis (Lu, Hsieh, and Liu2016), a link connectivity measurement in the network. The identified trajectory is based on the maximum summation of knowledge flow in the entire network (Verspagen2005). Thus, patents on the trajectory are the milestones to a field, and it is hard to skip them as developing technologies.

In the focal actor element, we define the focal actor as a player whose technologies are widely adopted or continually improved by third parties. We rely on the clique algorithm to find the focal actors. A clique is a complete subgraph that a pair of nodes that has at least one link, such as a triangle, which is the smallest clique. In other studies, the clique has been utilising to investigate innovative productivity (Gomez Solorzano, Tortoriello, and Soda2019), social behaviours, such as bully behaviours (Lodder et al.2016), social cohesion (Moody and White2003). In our case, we first transform the technology citation network into a weighted assignee network. Then, we investigate the assignees in the largest clique by the R igraph package (Csardi and Nepusz2006) as the focal actors in clusters.

We arrange general, technological cluster, focal actor and technological trajectory analysis to reflect the framework of the multi-level perspective from top to bottom. The boundaries between levels are ambiguous due to the dynamic progress and mutual influence (Geels

). Thus, we focus on the analytical contents and their backgrounds instead of classifying the analytical results in which levels specifically. This operation allows us to observe the social-technical system flexibly and effectively, which also fits the understanding of the prior studies. Further illustrations between empirical analysis and the social-technical system are presented in the conclusion & discussion section.

As shown inFigure 2, quantum dot technology is essential to the quantum ecosystem, influencing other quantum technologies, such as quantum communication, quantum computation and quantum dot display. Quantum dot technology originates from the first quantum revolution. Since quantum dots emit monochromatic light, the most famous application is that they can improve the efficiency of light-emitting diodes (LED), or even more, they can be directly manufactured as displays (e.g. television display). Quantum dots also have quantum effects such as coherence and entanglement, so they have applications in the second quantum revolution, such as quantum computation (Loss and DiVincenzo1998). The age of the quantum dot display cluster is 8.44, which is much younger than the age of the photodetector & semiconductor light-emitting devices cluster, which implies that the quantum dot technologies provide another life cycle of the display market. The quantum computation cluster should be the most eye-catching one due to its youngest age and relatively high number of patents among the four main clusters. That is, quantum computation won most public awareness and grew rapidly in recent years.

Northrop Grumman developed two paths and then integrated the routes on the trajectory. After the patent of IBM (patent number: 8642998), the applications of the technologies are divided into two paths. Rigetti Computing Inc dominates one, and IBM dominates the other. Note that IBM also had granted patents, such as patent 10398031, patent 10629797, which referenced the patents of Rigetti Computing Inc.

Based on the collection positions on the trajectory and counts of patents, we infer that IBM is relatively critical to quantum computation and the standardisation activities in the future. Because technologies of IBM had referenced most of the other assignees, such as D-Wave Systems, Northrop Grumman, Rigetti Computing Inc., the computation system of IBM should be the most compatible (interoperable) to different system designs.

## Summary of the quantum ecosystem

We present the main subfields of quantum technologies and relevant actors interconnectedly in a quantum ecosystem. Generally, academic institutes are important knowledge providers. Technologies in the first revolution include quantum dot display and quantum dot technology, and the technologies in the second revolution include quantum computation, quantum communication and quantum sensing. While most of the focal assignees in the first revolution are from the Asian area, focal assignees in the second revolution are from the North American area. Quantum computation is the most competitive field due to its high knowledge density and continual refinement of the prior arts. Note that the different physical principles behind the revolutions lead to the discontinuity to the competitive advantages, and thus it may be the new opportunities for some new economies.

## Insights from social-technical system

From the lens of the social-technical system, the analytical contents inFigure 1,Figure 2, andFigure 3somehow reflect the phenomena at the social-technical landscape level. That is, quantum technologies obtain attention increasingly in the recent three years. The main categories of quantum technologies include quantum communication, quantum computation, quantum dot technology, quantum dot display, and quantum sensing technology, which corresponds to the official reports (Acín et al.2018; National Science and Technology Council (U.S.)2018). Thus, it is straightforward to accept that actors inFigure 3, such as IBM, D-Wave systems, Samsung, etc., are focal to quantum technologies in general.

The focal actors could be at the regime level and niche level at the same time. For instance, IBM is one focal actor in the current regime level, which dominates some technologies in the classic computer field or the quantum dot technology, as shown inFigure 4. Meanwhile, IBM is also one focal actor in quantum computation trajectory, as shown inFigure 5, which should be at the technological niche level in the current background.

A focal actor stays at different level in different field. For instance, Samsung is decisive in the quantum dot display field but do not have many involvements in the quantum communication field. If the quantum dot display technology becomes the mainstream technology on the display devices in the future, then Samsung should be one focal actor at the social-technical regime level, which is likely to be the provider of standard-like specifications in the industry. However, if the case is the quantum computation, Samsung is less likely to be the focal actors in the social-technical regime level. In short, the level and the importance of an actor depend on the analytical contents and its background.

Last, we demonstrate a technological trajectory of quantum computation as the instance in the technological niche level. Our analysis supports that quantum computation is likely to transform the world massively due to its high and intensive resource engagement in recent years. Those actors who are interested in quantum computation should not avoid the knowledge on the trajectory and possible litigation issues in the future. At the same time, those actors who are not involved in quantum computation should also notice the progress due to its spill-over effects on other technologies.

## A path to be a focal actor

The acknowledgement of quantum supremacy increases the coopetition in the international world. Due to the data limitation, we do not mention the situation in other countries, especially China. China has invested many resources in quantum science, especially in quantum communication. In 2017, China launched a satellite, Mozi, for quantum experiments, and then Chinese scientists published an article on Nature, which is about the design and test on large scale information transmission (Gibney2016). An official publication from the European Union also showed that China applied for patents aggressively than most countries in subfields of quantum information, including quantum key distribution, entanglement, cold-atom interferometry (Travagnin2019). China is undoubtedly an ambitious player in quantum information. However, readers may wonder why our analytical results only reveal focal actors from the US, Canada and Japan, etc., without actors from China.7Here we provide possible explanations on the missing of China as a focal country. First, our patent data is limited to a single country scope, and we only select granted patents instead of application patents. In our dataset, China ranks the 10 th place of 1,531 granted patents with top quantum keywords, which is less than 100 patents. Generally, the United States focus on quantum computation. However, China puts more emphasis on quantum communication. These reasons may explain why we have fewer granted patents from China in the data pool.

Second, China is still a young player in (quantum) science, and it needs time and continual investment to be the focal actors in the ecosystem. Our study defines the focal actors as the players whose technologies are widely adopted or continually improved by third parties. In other words, it is not enough to apply for patents aggressively, and it also needs time to win recognition, such as granted patents, increasing forward citation from other players. Simultaneously, quantum applications are still in the exploring stage, which means that it is not clear to find out the best practice currently, and players are flexible to search or make better solutions. These reasons lead to the value uncertainty of patents from China, and it needs time to verify the role of China and its patent values.

Last, although technology is the prime importance in the multi-level perspective framework (Müller and Blanquart2018), patent data as a presentation of the technology element is still a part of the entire social-technical system. Since there is no comprehensive data, which record all the events in the social-technical system (Geels2019), we suggest that it may be more accessible to use several representative data, such as patents and others, simultaneously to investigate the transition in society.
