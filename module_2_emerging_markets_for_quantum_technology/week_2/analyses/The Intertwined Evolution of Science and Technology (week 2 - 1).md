# The Intertwined Evolution of Science and Technology (week 2 - 1)

To master the concepts from Week 2 regarding the nature of science and technology, focus on their distinct functional structures, their historical evolution, and the shifting norms that govern them.

### Main Features of Modern Science and Technology

\*\*Modern science\*\* is defined as a systematic process of generating generalizable and replicable knowledge about the natural and human-made world \[1\]. Key features include:
\*   \*\*Methodical Empiricism:\*\* It relies on collecting data, testing hypotheses, and drawing unbiased conclusions through peer-reviewed processes \[1, 2\].
\*   \*\*Direction of Fit (Mind-to-World):\*\* Scientific theories are designed to be "true" by matching how the world actually is; when they do not match, the theories are changed to fit the world \[3, 4\].
\*   \*\*Exponential Growth:\*\* Science has grown exponentially since the 1940s, doubling in output roughly every nine years \[5\].
\*   \*\*Predictive Nature:\*\* Science ideally uses known starting conditions (causes) to predict unknown end results (effects) \[6\].

\*\*Technology\*\*, by contrast, comprises physical artefacts and the person-embodied knowledge required to design and build them to perform useful functions \[7, 8\]. Main features include:
\*   \*\*Direction of Fit (World-to-Mind):\*\* Technology starts with a human idea or design (the mind) and transforms the world to match that idea \[3, 4, 9\].
\*   \*\*Imposed Functions:\*\* Functions are not intrinsic physical properties; they are observer-relative meanings assigned by humans (e.g., a stone is only a "paperweight" because someone uses it as one) \[10-12\].
\*   \*\*Operational Principles:\*\* Technology is built on "operational principles"—the knowledge that "phenomena \*x\* can be generated using \*y\*"—which often originate outside of scientific theory \[13, 14\].
\*   \*\*Tacit Knowledge:\*\* Much of technology is non-codified and is learned through face-to-face mentoring and hands-on manipulation of physical prototypes \[15-18\].

### Interaction and Mutual Contribution

Science and technology are \*\*distinct but dancing partners\*\* that have come closer together over time \[19\].
\*   \*\*Technology Preceding Science:\*\* Historically, technology often developed without scientific input. For example, the Wright brothers achieved flight before aerodynamics existed, and steam engines were used widely before thermodynamics was developed \[20, 21\].
\*   \*\*Technology’s Contribution to Science:\*\* Technology provides science with essential \*\*instrumentation and tools\*\* (e.g., telescopes for astronomy, computers for modeling) and defines new \*\*problem spaces\*\* (e.g., the need to understand semiconductors arose after the transistor was built) \[19, 22\].
\*   \*\*Science’s Contribution to Technology:\*\* Modern science provides \*\*background knowledge\*\*, specialized \*\*training for workers\*\*, and a means to reduce the number of costly "trial and error" redesign cycles in complex engineering \[23-25\].
\*   \*\*Purification and Convergence:\*\* Modern industry "purifies" materials and processes so they behave more like the simplified models used in scientific theory, while science uses "models" (abstract technologies) to manage the complexity of the world \[26-28\].

### Differentiation: Purpose, Practice, and Structure

| Feature | Science | Technology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Purpose\*\* | To produce codified \*\*theories and models\*\* that explain and predict natural reality \[29, 30\]. | To design and develop \*\*useful, producible artefacts\*\* that satisfy human needs \[29, 30\]. |
| \*\*Practice\*\* | Uses \*\*purified laboratory conditions\*\* to simplify variables and ensure analytical tractability \[31, 32\]. | Operates in the \*\*messy, complex real world\*\* where reliability for wide ranges of users is essential \[30, 33\]. |
| \*\*Institutional Structure\*\* | Centered in \*\*universities\*\*; success is determined by peer-reviewed publication and grants \[2, 34\]. | Centered in \*\*Business R&D\*\*; success is measured by commercial profit and product "fitness for use" \[29, 30\]. |
| \*\*Liability\*\* | Scientists are generally \*\*not legally responsible\*\* for the robustness of their findings in all contexts \[31, 32\]. | Engineers are \*\*professionally and legally responsible\*\* for the failure of the products they design \[30, 33\]. |

### Evaluation of Scientific Norms: 1930s vs. Today

The scientific norms established by \*\*Robert Merton\*\* in the 1940s (CUDOS) are increasingly at odds with the modern "Big Science" era \[35, 36\].

1.  \*\*CUDOS Norms (1930s/40s):\*\*
    \*   \*\*Communism:\*\* Open sharing of knowledge as a collective enterprise \[35\].
    \*   \*\*Universalism:\*\* Evaluation based solely on merit, free of personal bias \[35\].
    \*   \*\*Disinterestedness:\*\* Research motivated by discovery, not profit or ambition \[35\].
    \*   \*\*Organised Scepticism:\*\* Critical scrutiny of all claims \[35\].

2.  \*\*PLACE Norms (Modern/Ziman):\*\*
    As science became "Big Science," it required massive funding and industrial links, leading to a new set of norms \[36, 37\]:
    \*   \*\*Proprietary:\*\* Knowledge is often owned by funders or universities \[37\].
    \*   \*\*Local:\*\* Focus is on specific, applied problems rather than universal truths \[37\].
    \*   \*\*Authoritarian:\*\* Managerial hierarchies guide research rather than total individual freedom \[37\].
    \*   \*\*Commissioned:\*\* Research is targeted to solve specific tasks for pay \[37\].
    \*   \*\*Expert:\*\* Deep specialized expertise is prioritized over broad creativity \[37\].

\*\*Relevance Evaluation:\*\* While Merton’s CUDOS norms remain an \*\*aspirational ideal\*\* that provides the "certified knowledge" society trusts, they are under severe strain \[35, 38\]. The pressure for "impact" and funding has led to modern pathologies, such as the temptation to \*\*hype results\*\*, a decline in genuine scepticism, and a focus on "fundable" rather than truly important or risky research \[39\]. Consequently, the \*\*PLACE norms\*\* more accurately describe the current institutional reality where science is a key driver of the "ethic of productivity" \[37, 40\].
