# The Divided Lab: Distinguishing Science from Technology (Week 3 -2)

Based on Keith Pavitt’s analysis in "What makes basic research economically useful?", here is a clarification of the differences between science and technology, their purposes, and the implications for their support and institutional placement.

### Main Differences Between Science and Technology
While science and technology share similar inputs—such as scientists, engineers, and laboratories—and both produce knowledge as an output, they are fundamentally different in their core activities \[1, 2\]. 
\*   \*\*Nature of Output:\*\* Science produces \*\*codified, published knowledge\*\* and reproducible experiments \[1\]. In contrast, technology involves \*\*person-embodied knowledge\*\* and physical artefacts \[3\].
\*   \*\*Knowledge Type:\*\* Scientific knowledge is generally \*\*generalizable and universal\*\*, whereas technological knowledge is \*\*specific to particular artefacts\*\* and often \*\*tacit (uncodifiable)\*\* \[1, 4\].
\*   \*\*Predictability:\*\* Science ideally moves from known starting conditions to predict unknown end results \[5\]. Technology starts with a known desired end result and works backward to find the initially unknown starting conditions (designs) that will achieve it \[5\].

### Differences in Main Purposes
According to Pavitt, the objectives of these two fields are distinct:
\*   \*\*Science:\*\* Its purpose is to produce \*\*theories and models\*\* that explain and predict natural reality \[6\]. To remain analytically tractable, science requires the \*\*simplification and reduction of variables\*\*, often performed under controlled laboratory conditions \[6, 7\].
\*   \*\*Technology:\*\* Its purpose is to \*\*design and develop producible and useful artefacts\*\* \[6\]. These artefacts are often so complex and involve so many interacting variables that formal scientific theory is a weak guide; therefore, knowledge is accumulated through \*\*trial and error and operating experience\*\* \[1, 6\].

### Implications for Support and Funding
The differences in purpose and the nature of the knowledge produced determine how society supports them:
\*   \*\*Public Subsidy for Science:\*\* Because basic research is a "public good" (codified and easily reproduced) and involves \*\*high uncertainty\*\* regarding when or where it might be applied, profit-seeking firms are less likely to invest in it \[8, 9\]. This justifies a \*\*higher proportion of public funding\*\* for science to prevent private under-investment \[8, 10\].
\*   \*\*Private Investment for Technology:\*\* In seeking potential applications, firms learn to combine research results with \*\*firm-specific assets\*\*, making the resulting technology harder for competitors to imitate overnight \[11\]. Because this knowledge is more specific and short-term, it is more appropriately supported by \*\*private business investment\*\* \[10\].

### Main Features of Technical Knowledge
Technical knowledge possesses several characteristics that set it apart from purely scientific information:
\*   \*\*Tacit and Uncodifiable:\*\* A significant portion of technical knowledge cannot be written down; it is learned through \*\*face-to-face mentoring\*\* and hands-on manipulation of prototypes \[1, 12\].
\*   \*\*Cumulative and Path-Dependent:\*\* It builds incrementally on previous designs and "operational principles" (the knowledge that "phenomena \*x\* can be generated using \*y\*") \[4, 13\].
\*   \*\*Context-Specific:\*\* It is often specific to a particular firm, product, or production process and is therefore \*\*difficult and costly to reproduce\*\* or transfer between organizations \[1, 4, 14\].

### Why Science is in Universities and Technology is in Firms
The institutional separation reflects the distinct "directions of fit" and reward structures of each field:
\*   \*\*Universities (Science):\*\* These institutions are suited for basic research because they prioritize \*\*generalization and openness\*\* \[1\]. Scientists are motivated by publication and peer evaluation, focusing on \*\*new discoveries\*\* rather than reliable, marketable products \[1, 15\].
\*   \*\*Firms (Technology):\*\* Firms are the primary site for technology development because they must accumulate knowledge on the \*\*critical operating variables\*\* of artefacts to ensure they are "fit for use" in the messy real world \[1, 16\]. Firms provide the \*\*complementary assets\*\* (like marketing and production facilities) necessary to capture the economic value created by these artefacts, which university structures are not designed to manage \[1, 17\].
