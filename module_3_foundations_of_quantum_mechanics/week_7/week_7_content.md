# Week 7: Quantum entanglement and composite systems
## Intro
One of the most intriguing and non-intuitive features of quantum mechanics is entanglement, which occurs when two or more particles become connected in such a way that the state of one particle is correlated with the state of the other(s), even when they are far apart. In this week, we will delve deeper into the concept of quantum entanglement and its properties, focusing specifically on composite systems made of two or more entangled particles.
By the end of this week, you will be able to:
mathematically describe composite systems
discuss and explain the concept of entanglement.

#### Problem 2

In the following question, we will be working with the state $|\psi\rangle$ that is made of two entangled particles. The measurement on each particle can give two possible values that we will denote $0$ and $1$ (we could note those two outcomes $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$, or $+$ and $-$, or 'Head' and 'Tail'; the notation doesn't matter). The entangled state $|\psi\rangle$ is defined as 

$$|\psi\rangle = A \left( \frac{1}{2} |00\rangle + \frac{1}{2} |01\rangle + \frac{1}{2} |11\rangle \right)$$

where $A$ is a normalisation constant.

1. Find the value of $A$ such that $|\psi\rangle$ is normalised.
2. You measure the state of the first particle and obtain a measurement of $|0\rangle$. What is the state after this measurement?
3. If you now measure the second qubit, what are the possible results and probabilities of those results?
4. Suppose instead you measure the first particle and obtain a measurement of $|1\rangle$.
   1. What is the state after this measurement?
   2. If you now measure the second particle, what are the possible results and probabilities of those results?
   3. Is $|\psi\rangle$ an entangled state? Justify your answer.

#### Problem 3

Consider the three composite spin operators $\sigma_x \otimes \mathbb{1}$, $\sigma_y \otimes \mathbb{1}$ and $\sigma_z \otimes \mathbb{1}$ acting on a general product state

$$|\psi\rangle = (a_1 |\uparrow\rangle + b_1 |\downarrow\rangle) \otimes (a_2 |\uparrow\rangle + b_2 |\downarrow\rangle)$$

Using the expression for kets and operators listed below, prove that when any of those three composite spin operators acts on a product state, the result is still a product state.

$$|\uparrow\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$|\downarrow\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\mathbb{1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

### Problems

#### Problem 1

Consider a two-spin composite state $|\psi\rangle$ prepared such that we can write

$$|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (|\uparrow_A \downarrow_B\rangle - |\downarrow_A \uparrow_B\rangle)$$

where, for clarity, the subscripts $A$ and $B$ denote the subspace on which Alice and Bob will measure their spin components. First, Bob measures $y$-component of the spin with the operator $\tau_y$, then Alice measures $x$-component with the operator $\sigma_x$.

1. What is the expectation value of $\langle \sigma_x \tau_y \rangle = \langle \psi | \sigma_x \tau_y | \psi \rangle$?
2. Calculate the expectation values of $\langle \tau_y \rangle = \langle \psi | \tau_y | \psi \rangle$ and $\langle \sigma_x \rangle = \langle \psi | \sigma_x | \psi \rangle$.
3. What is the correlation between the two measurements ($\langle \sigma_x \tau_y \rangle - \langle \sigma_x \rangle \langle \tau_y \rangle$)?
