# Glossary — Module 3 Foundations Sprint

Notation and vocabulary in one place. Each entry: term or symbol, how to say it aloud, one plain sentence, a tiny example, and where to see it in the days and companions. "Commonly confused with" appears only where a real trap exists. Where a meaning is a convention rather than something the lecture states, the entry says **(convention)** or flags it in the text. Tiny examples use small made-up objects, never the course's own problems.

Conventions used throughout: the inner product conjugates the left object and is linear in the ket (Eqs. 2.8, 2.9, 2.11); the adjoint is defined by $\langle\psi|\hat A\phi\rangle=\langle\hat A^\dagger\psi|\phi\rangle$ (Eq. 2.14); ladder operators follow lecture Eqs. 5.6-5.11; Pauli matrices have eigenvalues $\pm1$ and the spin component $\hat S_j=\tfrac\hbar2\sigma_j$ is a **(convention)** not stated in the lecture.

## Day 1 — Complex numbers and trig triage

Everything here feeds the modulus-squared rule that makes probabilities.

- <a id="complex-number"></a>**complex number** — *say it as:* "complex number" — *meaning:* A number $a+ib$ with a real part $a$ and an imaginary part $b$, which you can picture as the point $(a,b)$. — *tiny example:* $3+4i$ is the point $(3,4)$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2
- <a id="imaginary-unit"></a>**i (imaginary unit)** — *say it as:* "i" — *meaning:* The number with $i^2=-1$. — *tiny example:* $i^3=-i$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2
- <a id="real-part"></a>**real part, Re z** — *say it as:* "real part of z" — *meaning:* The $a$ in $a+ib$; a real number. — *tiny example:* $\mathrm{Re}(2-5i)=2$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2
- <a id="imaginary-part"></a>**imaginary part, Im z** — *say it as:* "imaginary part of z" — *meaning:* The $b$ in $a+ib$ (not $ib$); a real number. — *tiny example:* $\mathrm{Im}(2-5i)=-5$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2 — *Commonly confused with:* real part vs imaginary part: the imaginary part is $b$, without the $i$.
- <a id="complex-conjugate"></a>**complex conjugate, z*** — *say it as:* "z star" — *meaning:* Flip the sign of every $i$; it reflects the point across the real axis. — *tiny example:* $(1+2i)^*=1-2i$; $(e^{i\theta})^*=e^{-i\theta}$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $z^*$ (conjugate) vs $z^2$ (square). Only $z^*z$ makes probabilities.
- <a id="modulus"></a>**modulus, |z|** — *say it as:* "mod z" — *meaning:* The length of $z$, its distance from the origin: $|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. — *tiny example:* $|3+4i|=5$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2
- <a id="mod-squared"></a>**|z|² = z*z** — *say it as:* "mod z squared" — *meaning:* The size squared of a complex number; real and never negative. This, not $z^2$, is what turns amplitudes into probabilities. — *tiny example:* $|1+i|^2=(1-i)(1+i)=2$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.2 — *Commonly confused with:* $|z|^2=z^*z$ (real, $\ge0$) vs $z^2$ (usually complex).
- <a id="phase"></a>**phase** — *say it as:* "phase" — *meaning:* The angle $\theta$ in $e^{i\theta}$: it carries direction but no size. — *tiny example:* $e^{i\pi/2}=i$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.3
- <a id="eulers-formula"></a>**Euler's formula** — *say it as:* "Euler's formula" — *meaning:* $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$; it links exponentials to angles and shows $|e^{i\theta}|=1$. — *tiny example:* $e^{i\pi}=-1$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.3
- <a id="unit-circle"></a>**unit circle** — *say it as:* "unit circle" — *meaning:* The set of complex numbers with $|z|=1$; every $e^{i\theta}$ sits on it. — *tiny example:* $e^{i\theta}$ for $\theta=0$ to $2\pi$ goes once round. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.3
- <a id="global-phase"></a>**global phase** — *say it as:* "global phase" — *meaning:* A factor $e^{i\gamma}$ multiplying a whole state; it changes no probability. — *tiny example:* $e^{i\gamma}\lvert\psi\rangle$ and $\lvert\psi\rangle$ predict the same results. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.3 — *Commonly confused with:* global phase (unmeasurable) vs relative phase between two terms of a superposition (measurable).
- <a id="pythagorean-identity"></a>**sin²θ + cos²θ = 1** — *say it as:* "sine squared plus cosine squared is one" — *meaning:* The basic trig identity; used to normalise two-component states written with angles. — *tiny example:* $\sin^2(0.3)+\cos^2(0.3)=1$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.4
- <a id="double-angle"></a>**double-angle identity** — *say it as:* "double angle" — *meaning:* $\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\cos2\theta=\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta$; turns products into single terms. — *tiny example:* $2\sin15^\circ\cos15^\circ=\sin30^\circ=\tfrac12$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.4
- <a id="half-angle"></a>**half-angle identity** — *say it as:* "half angle" — *meaning:* $\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12(1-\cos2\theta)$ and $\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12(1+\cos2\theta)$; the tool for integrating $\sin^2$. — *tiny example:* $\sin^2 45^\circ=\tfrac12(1-0)=\tfrac12$. — *see:* [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.4; [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.2

## Day 2 — Vectors, bra-ket notation and inner products

Notation first: bras, kets and the overlap bracket.

- <a id="vector"></a>**vector** — *say it as:* "vector" — *meaning:* An ordered list of numbers (components) that can be added and scaled; states are vectors. — *tiny example:* $(1,\,2)$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.1
- <a id="column-vector"></a>**column vector** — *say it as:* "column vector" — *meaning:* A vector written as a vertical stack; the matrix form of a ket. — *tiny example:* $\begin{pmatrix}1\\ i\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.1
- <a id="dot-product"></a>**dot product, length** — *say it as:* "dot product" — *meaning:* Sum of products of components for real vectors; the length is $\sqrt{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v}$. Complex vectors need the bra (conjugate) version instead. — *tiny example:* $(1,2)\cdot(3,-1)=1$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.1
- <a id="ket"></a>**|ψ⟩ (ket)** — *say it as:* "ket psi" — *meaning:* The state written as a column vector; $|\ \rangle$ says "column". — *tiny example:* $\lvert u\rangle=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.2; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* bra vs ket: the ket is the column, the bra is the conjugated row.
- <a id="bra"></a>**⟨ψ| (bra)** — *say it as:* "bra psi" — *meaning:* The conjugate transpose of the ket: turn the column into a row and conjugate every entry. — *tiny example:* $\lvert a\rangle=(i,2)^{\mathsf T}\Rightarrow\langle a\rvert=(-i,\ 2)$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.2; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* bra vs ket: forgetting to conjugate when you form the bra is the classic slip.
- <a id="inner-product"></a>**⟨φ|ψ⟩ (inner product)** — *say it as:* "bra phi ket psi"; "the overlap of phi with psi" — *meaning:* Bra times ket: one complex number. Convention used throughout: conjugate the left object, multiply, then sum (vectors) or integrate (functions). — *tiny example:* $\langle\varphi\lvert\psi\rangle=\int\varphi^*\psi\,\mathrm{d}x$ (Eq. 2.8). — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.2; [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.1; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle$ vs $\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle$: they are complex conjugates of each other, not equal.
- <a id="linearity-ket"></a>**linearity in the ket** — *say it as:* "linear in the ket" — *meaning:* Constants and sums come straight out of the ket slot: $\langle\psi|\alpha\phi_1+\beta\phi_2\rangle=\alpha\langle\psi|\phi_1\rangle+\beta\langle\psi|\phi_2\rangle$ (Eq. 2.9). — *tiny example:* $\langle A|3B\rangle=3\langle A|B\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.3 — *Commonly confused with:* ket slot (linear) vs bra slot (anti-linear).
- <a id="conjugate-symmetry"></a>**conjugate symmetry (interchange rule)** — *say it as:* "swap gives conjugate" — *meaning:* Interchanging bra and ket conjugates the number: $\langle B|A\rangle=\langle A|B\rangle^*$ (Eq. 2.11). — *tiny example:* If $\langle A|B\rangle=2+i$ then $\langle B|A\rangle=2-i$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.3
- <a id="anti-linear"></a>**anti-linear (bra slot)** — *say it as:* "anti-linear" — *meaning:* Constants leave the bra slot conjugated: $\langle\alpha A|B\rangle=\alpha^*\langle A|B\rangle$ (Eq. 2.10). — *tiny example:* $\langle iA|B\rangle=-i\langle A|B\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.3
- <a id="positivity"></a>**positivity, ⟨A|A⟩ ≥ 0** — *say it as:* "the norm-squared is non-negative" — *meaning:* A vector's overlap with itself is real and $\ge0$, zero only for the zero vector (Eq. 2.7). — *tiny example:* $\langle a|a\rangle=|i|^2+|2|^2=5$ for $(i,2)^{\mathsf T}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.3
- <a id="orthogonal"></a>**orthogonal** — *say it as:* "orthogonal" — *meaning:* Two states are orthogonal when their inner product is $0$: perpendicular, no overlap. — *tiny example:* $\langle 0|1\rangle=0$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4
- <a id="orthonormal"></a>**orthonormal** — *say it as:* "orthonormal" — *meaning:* Orthogonal and each of length 1; packed into $\langle n|m\rangle=\delta_{nm}$. — *tiny example:* $\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md) — *Commonly confused with:* orthogonal (just perpendicular) vs orthonormal (perpendicular and normalised).
- <a id="kronecker-delta"></a>**δₙₘ (Kronecker delta)** — *say it as:* "Kronecker delta n m" — *meaning:* $1$ if $n=m$, $0$ otherwise. — *tiny example:* $\delta_{23}=0$, $\delta_{22}=1$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="basis"></a>**basis** — *say it as:* "basis" — *meaning:* A set of vectors you can build every state from; an orthonormal basis makes the coefficients easy to read off. — *tiny example:* $\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle$ for a qubit. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4
- <a id="expansion-coefficient"></a>**expansion coefficient cₙ (αₙ)** — *say it as:* "c n" ("alpha n" in the lecture) — *meaning:* The weight of basis state $|n\rangle$ in $|\psi\rangle=\sum_nc_n|n\rangle$: $c_n=\langle n|\psi\rangle$. The lecture writes it $\alpha_n$. — *tiny example:* $\lvert\psi\rangle=\tfrac35\lvert 0\rangle+\tfrac45\lvert 1\rangle$ gives $c_0=\tfrac35$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* amplitude $c_n$ vs probability $|c_n|^2$.
- <a id="normalisation"></a>**normalisation, normalised state** — *say it as:* "normalised" — *meaning:* A state is normalised when its total probability is 1: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_n|c_n|^2=1$. — *tiny example:* $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert0\rangle+\lvert1\rangle)$ is normalised. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.4; [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.5 — *Commonly confused with:* normalised (already 1) vs normalisable (some constant makes it 1).
- <a id="matrix"></a>**matrix** — *say it as:* "matrix" — *meaning:* A rectangular grid of numbers; in this course, $2\times2$ grids that act as operators. — *tiny example:* $\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&5\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.5
- <a id="matrix-vector-product"></a>**matrix–vector product** — *say it as:* "matrix times vector" — *meaning:* Each output entry is a row of the matrix dotted with the column; this is how an operator acts on a ket. — *tiny example:* $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}b\\a\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.5; [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.1
- <a id="matrix-multiplication"></a>**matrix multiplication** — *say it as:* "matrix product" — *meaning:* Rows times columns; the order matters, $AB\ne BA$ in general. — *tiny example:* $\sigma_x\sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ but $\sigma_z\sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md), Move 2.5

## Day 3 — Operators, eigenproblems, Hermitian matrices and the Pauli matrices

The eigen-vocabulary and the adjoint/Hermitian pair; the source of most vocabulary traps.

- <a id="operator"></a>**operator, Ô (Â)** — *say it as:* "A hat" — *meaning:* A rule that turns a state into a state; a matrix on kets, a differential rule on functions. A hat means operator (except the unit vector $\hat{\mathbf e}_x$). — *tiny example:* $\hat x\psi=x\psi$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.1; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\hat A$ (operator) vs the number $A$ or $a_n$ it returns.
- <a id="eigenvalue-equation"></a>**eigenvalue equation Â|φ⟩ = λ|φ⟩** — *say it as:* "A hat on phi equals lambda phi" — *meaning:* The operator only rescales this state and leaves its direction alone. — *tiny example:* $\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&5\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}=5\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="eigenvalue"></a>**eigenvalue** — *say it as:* "eigenvalue" — *meaning:* The number $\lambda$ that comes out when the operator acts on its eigenvector; a possible measurement result. — *tiny example:* Above: $\lambda=2$ or $5$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2 — *Commonly confused with:* eigenvalue (number) vs eigenvector/eigenstate (the state that carries it).
- <a id="eigenvector"></a>**eigenvector** — *say it as:* "eigenvector" — *meaning:* The direction (column) the operator leaves fixed, up to the factor $\lambda$. — *tiny example:* $(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$ above. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2 — *Commonly confused with:* eigenvector vs eigenvalue vs eigenstate: vector and state are the same object (state is the physics name); the eigenvalue is the number.
- <a id="eigenstate"></a>**eigenstate** — *say it as:* "eigenstate" — *meaning:* An eigenvector viewed as a physical state: a definite value of the observable. — *tiny example:* $\lvert\phi_1\rangle$ belongs to $\lambda_1$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* eigenstate vs eigenvalue: measuring in an eigenstate gives its eigenvalue for certain.
- <a id="eigenfunction"></a>**eigenfunction** — *say it as:* "eigenfunction" — *meaning:* An eigenvector that is a function of $x$. — *tiny example:* $e^{ikx}$ for $\hat p$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.8; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="characteristic-polynomial"></a>**characteristic polynomial** — *say it as:* "characteristic polynomial" — *meaning:* $\det(A-\lambda\mathbb 1)$ set equal to 0; solving it gives the eigenvalues. — *tiny example:* For $\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&d\end{pmatrix}$: $(a-\lambda)(d-\lambda)=0$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2
- <a id="degenerate"></a>**degenerate** — *say it as:* "degenerate" — *meaning:* Two or more independent eigenvectors share one eigenvalue. — *tiny example:* $\mathbb 1$ has eigenvalue 1 twice. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2
- <a id="trace-det"></a>**trace and determinant checks** — *say it as:* "trace, determinant" — *meaning:* For a $2\times2$: trace $=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$, determinant $=\lambda_1\lambda_2$; a fast check on eigenvalues. — *tiny example:* $\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma_z=0$, $\det\sigma_z=-1$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.2
- <a id="adjoint"></a>**adjoint, † (dagger)** — *say it as:* "A dagger" — *meaning:* The operator with $\langle\psi|\hat A\phi\rangle=\langle\hat A^\dagger\psi|\phi\rangle$ (lecture Eq. 2.14); for matrices, the conjugate transpose. - <a id="hermitian"></a>**Hermitian** — *say it as:* "Hermitian" — *meaning:* An operator equal to its own adjoint, $\hat A^\dagger=\hat A$ (Eq. 2.13); guarantees real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenstates for different eigenvalues. — *tiny example:* $\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&1\end{pmatrix}$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.3; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), skipped steps C — *Commonly confused with:* Hermitian vs adjoint: every operator has an adjoint, only some equal it. $\hat a^\dagger$ is the adjoint of $\hat a$ but neither is Hermitian.
- <a id="observable"></a>**observable** — *say it as:* "observable" — *meaning:* A measurable quantity, represented by a Hermitian operator whose eigenvalues are the possible results. — *tiny example:* Energy: $\hat H$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.3; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md)
- <a id="pauli-matrices"></a>**σₓ, σ_y, σ_z (Pauli matrices)** — *say it as:* "sigma x, sigma y, sigma z" — *meaning:* Three $2\times2$ Hermitian matrices with eigenvalues $\pm1$: $\sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $\sigma_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}$, $\sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$. Spin component $\hat S_j=\tfrac\hbar2\sigma_j$ is a convention (not in the lecture). — *tiny example:* $\sigma_x\lvert{\uparrow}\rangle=\lvert{\downarrow}\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.4; [Week 6 companion](companions/week06_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\sigma_j$ (eigenvalues $\pm1$) vs $\hat S_j$ (eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$). — *Physics view:* they describe the spin-½ beams and the sequential Stern-Gerlach chains ([P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.5).
- <a id="spin-up-down"></a>**spin-up/spin-down, |↑⟩, |↓⟩ (|u⟩, |d⟩)** — *say it as:* "ket up, ket down" — *meaning:* The two $z$-eigenstates; as columns (convention) $\lvert{\uparrow}\rangle=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$, $\lvert{\downarrow}\rangle=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}$. $|u\rangle,|d\rangle$ appear in the Week 3 Task (not the lecture); the Week 6 lecture writes $|\psi_z^\pm\rangle$ (and $|\psi_x^\pm\rangle$): same states, mapping as in Day 3. — *tiny example:* $\sigma_z\lvert{\downarrow}\rangle=-\lvert{\downarrow}\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.4; [Week 6 companion](companions/week06_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $|\uparrow\rangle$ (a state) vs the outcome $+$ (a measured result).
- <a id="x-y-states"></a>**|±x⟩, |±y⟩** — *say it as:* "plus x, minus x" — *meaning:* Eigenstates of $\sigma_x,\sigma_y$: $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,\pm1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,\pm i)^{\mathsf T}$. Column form is the standard convention. — *tiny example:* Each is a 50/50 mix in the $z$ basis. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.4; [Week 6 companion](companions/week06_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="commutator"></a>**commutator [Â, B̂] = ÂB̂ − B̂Â** — *say it as:* "commutator of A and B" — *meaning:* Zero when the order of the two operators does not matter; non-zero when it does. — *tiny example:* $[\sigma_z,\sigma_z]=0$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.5; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), decoder — *Commonly confused with:* commutator $[A,B]=AB-BA$ vs anticommutator $\{A,B\}=AB+BA$ (convention; the anticommutator is not used in the course files).
- <a id="anticommutator"></a>**anticommutator {Â, B̂}** — *say it as:* "anticommutator" — *meaning:* (convention) The sum $AB+BA$ instead of the difference. Not used by the course; listed only to warn you off mixing the two signs. — *tiny example:* $\{\sigma_x,\sigma_x\}=2\cdot\mathbb 1$. — *see:* [Day 3](day03.md), Move 3.5 — *Commonly confused with:* [A,B] (minus) vs {A,B} (plus).

## Day 4 — Calculus as the module uses it

Functions as vectors: the sum becomes an integral.

- <a id="wave-function"></a>**wave function ψ** — *say it as:* "psi of x" — *meaning:* A complex number for each position (and time) that specifies the state (Postulate 1). — *tiny example:* $\psi(x)=Ce^{-|x|}$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md); [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="psi-star"></a>**ψ* (psi star)** — *say it as:* "psi star" — *meaning:* The complex conjugate of the wave function; needed in every inner product. — *tiny example:* $(e^{ikx})^*=e^{-ikx}$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.1; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="probability-density"></a>**|ψ|² = ψ*ψ (probability density)** — *say it as:* "mod psi squared" — *meaning:* Probability per unit length at $x$: real and $\ge0$. — *tiny example:* $|e^{ikx}|^2=1$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.6; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\psi$ (complex amplitude) vs $|\psi|^2$ (probability density). Forgetting the star is the classic slip.
- <a id="probability-amplitude"></a>**probability amplitude** — *say it as:* "amplitude" — *meaning:* The complex number ($\psi(x)$, $c_n$, $\langle b|a\rangle$) whose modulus squared is a probability. — *tiny example:* Amplitude $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$ gives probability $\tfrac12$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.1; [Week 6 companion](companions/week06_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* probability amplitude (complex) vs probability (real, in $[0,1]$). — *Physics view:* the probability amplitude (physical) is the number attached to each place or route, and amplitudes for indistinguishable routes add before squaring ([P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.5).
- <a id="normalisable"></a>**normalisable** — *say it as:* "normalisable" — *meaning:* $\int|\psi|^2\mathrm dx$ is finite, so some constant can make it 1. — *tiny example:* $e^{-x^2}$ yes; $e^{ikx}$ (plane wave) no. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.5; [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.7 — *Commonly confused with:* normalisable vs normalised.
- <a id="normalisation-constant"></a>**normalisation constant** — *say it as:* "normalisation constant, C" — *meaning:* The constant $C$ chosen so $\int|\psi|^2\mathrm dx=1$. — *tiny example:* $\int_0^1C^2\,\mathrm dx=1$ gives $C=1$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.5; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), Clone 3
- <a id="expectation-value"></a>**expectation value ⟨x⟩, ⟨Â⟩** — *say it as:* "expectation of x" — *meaning:* The average result over many identical preparations: $\langle x\rangle=\int\psi^*x\psi\,\mathrm dx$. — *tiny example:* Uniform density on $[0,2]$: $\langle x\rangle=1$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.6; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* expectation (an average, not seen on one shot) vs the most likely value.
- <a id="variance"></a>**variance** — *say it as:* "variance" — *meaning:* $\langle x^2\rangle-\langle x\rangle^2$: the mean squared scatter. — *tiny example:* Sharp state: variance $0$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.6
- <a id="standard-deviation"></a>**standard deviation, σₓ, Δx** — *say it as:* "sigma x", "delta x" — *meaning:* The square root of the variance: the typical spread of results. Zero exactly in an eigenstate. — *tiny example:* Wide packet: large $\sigma_x$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.6; [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.3; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="gaussian"></a>**Gaussian** — *say it as:* "Gaussian" — *meaning:* A bell curve $e^{-ax^2}$; the standard integral is $\int e^{-ax^2}\mathrm dx=\sqrt{\pi/a}$. — *tiny example:* $\int e^{-x^2}\mathrm dx=\sqrt\pi$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.4
- <a id="odd-even"></a>**odd/even function** — *say it as:* "odd, even" — *meaning:* Even: $f(-x)=f(x)$; odd: $f(-x)=-f(x)$. An odd integrand over a symmetric range integrates to $0$. — *tiny example:* $x$ odd, $x^2$ even. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.4
- <a id="integration-by-parts"></a>**integration by parts** — *say it as:* "by parts" — *meaning:* $\int u\,\mathrm dv=uv-\int v\,\mathrm du$; boundary terms vanish for normalisable $\psi$. — *tiny example:* $\int xe^{-x}\mathrm dx$ from $0$ to $\infty$ is $1$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.3
- <a id="position-operator"></a>**position operator x̂** — *say it as:* "x hat" — *meaning:* Multiplies the wave function by $x$. — *tiny example:* $\hat x\psi=x\psi$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md); [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="momentum-operator"></a>**momentum operator p̂** — *say it as:* "p hat" — *meaning:* $\hat p=\frac\hbar i\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}=-i\hbar\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}$: differentiate and multiply by $-i\hbar$. — *tiny example:* $\hat p\,e^{ikx}=\hbar k\,e^{ikx}$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.8; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Physics view:* it is the classical $p=mv$ promoted to an operator, and on a wave $e^{ikx}$ it returns $\hbar k$ ([P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.5; [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.6).
- <a id="plane-wave"></a>**plane wave e^{ikx}** — *say it as:* "e to the i k x" — *meaning:* The momentum eigenfunction with $p=\hbar k$; not normalisable. — *tiny example:* $k=2$: $\hat p$ gives $2\hbar$. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.8; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder — *Physics view:* $e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ is the complex form of a travelling wave with wavenumber $k$ and angular frequency $\omega$, and its $|\cdot|^2=1$ everywhere ([P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.3).
- <a id="acceptable-wf"></a>**acceptable wave function** — *say it as:* "acceptable" — *meaning:* Finite, continuous, single-valued, with continuous derivative (for finite potentials) and normalisable. — *tiny example:* A step function fails continuity. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.7; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), Clone 3
- <a id="single-valued"></a>**single-valued** — *say it as:* "single-valued" — *meaning:* One value of $\psi$ at each $x$. — *tiny example:* A curve that doubles back is not a function. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.7
- <a id="continuous"></a>**continuous** — *say it as:* "continuous" — *meaning:* No jumps (and, for finite potentials, no kinks in $\psi$). — *tiny example:* $|x|$ continuous but has a kink at 0. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.7
- <a id="hbar"></a>**ħ (h-bar)** — *say it as:* "h-bar" — *meaning:* Planck's constant over $2\pi$; sets the scale of quantum effects. — *tiny example:* $\hat p$ has $\hbar$ in it. — *see:* [Day 4](day04.md), Move 4.8; [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder — *Physics view:* the lecture's $\hbar=h/2\pi=1.055\times10^{-34}$ J s is the reduced Planck's constant, an action in J s that pairs with $\omega$ and $k$ (while $h$ pairs with $f$ and $\lambda$) ([P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4).
- <a id="hilbert-space"></a>**Hilbert space, L²** — *say it as:* "Hilbert space, L two" — *meaning:* The space of functions with $\int|\psi|^2<\infty$; wave functions live here. — *tiny example:* Normalisable functions. — *see:* [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="d3r"></a>**d³r (volume element)** — *say it as:* "d three r" — *meaning:* A tiny volume $\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy\,\mathrm dz$; in 1D just $\mathrm dx$. — *tiny example:* $\int|\psi|^2\mathrm d^3\mathbf r=1$ (Eq. 2.1). — *see:* [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md), notation decoder

## Day 5 — Measurement, the Schrödinger equation and stationary states

- <a id="measurement-postulate"></a>**measurement postulate (Born rule in the eigenbasis)** — *say it as:* "measurement postulate" — *meaning:* Measuring $\hat A$ returns one eigenvalue $a_n$ with probability $|c_n|^2$. — *tiny example:* $c_n=\tfrac35$: chance $\tfrac9{25}$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.1; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), skipped steps A — *Commonly confused with:* $P(a_n)=|c_n|^2$ (probability) vs $c_n$ (amplitude).
- <a id="collapse"></a>**collapse** — *say it as:* "collapse" — *meaning:* After result $a_n$ the state is the matching eigenvector (renormalised); an immediate repeat gives the same answer. — *tiny example:* After outcome $-$ the state is $\lvert{\downarrow}\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.3; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), skipped steps D
- <a id="expectation-discrete"></a>**expectation value (discrete)** — *say it as:* "expectation, discrete" — *meaning:* $\langle A\rangle=\sum_n|c_n|^2a_n=\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle$. — *tiny example:* Outcomes $1$ and $3$ at $\tfrac12$ each: $2$ (not a possible result). — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.2; [Week 3 companion](companions/week03_companion.md), skipped steps B
- <a id="hamiltonian"></a>**Hamiltonian Ĥ** — *say it as:* "H hat" — *meaning:* The energy operator, kinetic plus potential; its eigenvalues are the possible energies. — *tiny example:* $\hat H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2+V(x)$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder — *Physics view:* $\hat H$ is the classical total energy $p^2/2m+V$ with $p$ replaced by $-i\hbar\,\partial/\partial x$ ([P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.5).
- <a id="schrodinger-equation"></a>**Schrödinger equation (time-dependent)** — *say it as:* "i h-bar partial t psi equals H psi" — *meaning:* $i\hbar\,\partial\Psi/\partial t=\hat H\Psi$: the law of how the state changes in time. — *tiny example:* On $e^{-i\omega t}$: $\hbar\omega\,e^{-i\omega t}$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="partial-t"></a>**∂/∂t (partial derivative)** — *say it as:* "partial t" — *meaning:* Rate of change in $t$ holding $x$ fixed; $\partial^2/\partial x^2$ is curvature in $x$. — *tiny example:* $\partial_x^2e^{ikx}=-k^2e^{ikx}$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="time-independent-se"></a>**time-independent Schrödinger equation** — *say it as:* "H psi equals E psi" — *meaning:* $\hat H\psi=E\psi$: the eigenvalue problem for energy. — *tiny example:* Solved in the box (Move 5.5). — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4 — *Commonly confused with:* time-dependent (evolves $\Psi$) vs time-independent (finds $\psi$ and $E$).
- <a id="capital-psi"></a>**Ψ(x,t) vs ψ(x)** — *say it as:* "capital psi", "small psi" — *meaning:* Capital: full time-dependent wave function. Small: spatial part only. — *tiny example:* $\Psi=e^{-i\omega t}\psi(x)$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\Psi(x,t)$ vs $\psi(x)$.
- <a id="separation-of-variables"></a>**separation of variables** — *say it as:* "separation of variables" — *meaning:* Guess $e^{-i\omega t}\psi(x)$ so the PDE splits into an ordinary equation in $x$. — *tiny example:* Gives $\hat H\psi=E\psi$ with $E=\hbar\omega$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), skipped steps A
- <a id="stationary-state"></a>**stationary state** — *say it as:* "stationary state" — *meaning:* A solution $\psi_n(x)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$: all probabilities are constant in time. — *tiny example:* $|\Psi|^2$ does not change. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), skipped steps B — *Commonly confused with:* stationary state (density constant) vs a superposition of energies (density moves).
- <a id="phase-factor"></a>**time phase e^{−iEt/ħ}** — *say it as:* "e to the minus i E t over h-bar" — *meaning:* A pure phase of modulus 1 that rotates at $\omega_n=E_n/\hbar$. — *tiny example:* $|e^{-i\theta}|=1$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.4; [Day 1](day01.md), Move 1.3
- <a id="time-evolution"></a>**time evolution** — *say it as:* "time evolution" — *meaning:* Each energy component just rotates its phase: $\Psi=\sum\alpha_n\psi_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$; compact form $e^{-i\hat Ht/\hbar}$ (time-evolution operator). — *tiny example:* $|\alpha_n|$ never change. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md); [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), Clone 3
- <a id="boundary-condition"></a>**boundary condition** — *say it as:* "boundary condition" — *meaning:* A requirement on $\psi$ at the edge of the region; it selects the allowed $k$. — *tiny example:* $\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5
- <a id="energy-level"></a>**energy level, Eₙ** — *say it as:* "E n" — *meaning:* An allowed energy (eigenvalue of $\hat H$); the list is the spectrum. — *tiny example:* Well: $E_n=n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/2mL^2$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder — *Physics view:* confinement lets only some standing waves fit, so only some wavelengths, hence some energies, are allowed ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3).
- <a id="potential-well"></a>**potential well** — *say it as:* "potential well" — *meaning:* A region of low $V$ that traps the particle; the infinite square well is the simplest. — *tiny example:* $V=0$ inside, $\infty$ outside. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5
- <a id="particle-in-box"></a>**particle in a box (infinite well)** — *say it as:* "particle in a box" — *meaning:* A particle with walls at $0$ and $L$: $\psi_n=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$. — *tiny example:* $n=1$ is the ground state. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), skipped steps C — *Physics view:* the standing-wave picture $\lambda_n=2L/n$ with $p=h/\lambda$ gives $E_n=n^2h^2/8mL^2$ without calculus ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3).
- <a id="quantised"></a>**quantised** — *say it as:* "quantised" — *meaning:* Only certain values are allowed, because of boundary conditions. — *tiny example:* Energies in a well. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5 — *Physics view:* the physical picture is quantisation (physical): confinement selects the allowed wavelengths ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3).
- <a id="time-not-observable"></a>**time is not an observable** — *say it as:* "time is a parameter" — *meaning:* In $\Psi(x,t)$, $t$ labels when; no Hermitian operator has times as eigenvalues. — *tiny example:* There is no $\hat t$. — *see:* [Day 5](day05.md)

## Day 6 — The oscillator toolkit

Ladder operators follow the lecture's Eqs. 5.6-5.11; not a textbook variant.

- <a id="harmonic-oscillator"></a>**harmonic oscillator** — *say it as:* "harmonic oscillator" — *meaning:* A system with a quadratic potential $V=\tfrac12m\omega^2x^2$; a good model near any potential minimum. — *tiny example:* A mass on a spring. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md); [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md) — *Physics view:* classically it is a mass on a spring with $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$; the quantum version keeps that $\omega$ as the ladder spacing $\hbar\omega$ ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.1; [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.5).

- <a id="xi"></a>**ξ (dimensionless variable)** — *say it as:* "xi" (ksee) — *meaning:* Position in natural units: $\xi=x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$, so constants drop out. — *tiny example:* $\hat a=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\xi+\mathrm d/\mathrm d\xi)$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.1; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="ladder-operators"></a>**ladder operators â, â†** — *say it as:* "a hat, a dagger" — *meaning:* Built from $\hat x,\hat p$ (lecture Eqs. 5.6, 5.7): $\hat a=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\big(\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,\hat x+i\hat p/\sqrt{m\hbar\omega}\big)$, $\hat a^\dagger$ has $-i$. They climb the energy ladder. — *tiny example:* $\hat a\lvert3\rangle=\sqrt3\lvert2\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* $\hat a$ vs $\hat a^\dagger$: adjoints, neither Hermitian.
- <a id="lowering"></a>**lowering operator â** — *say it as:* "a hat" — *meaning:* Removes one quantum; $\hat a\lvert n\rangle=\sqrt n\lvert n-1\rangle$, and $\hat a\lvert0\rangle=0$. — *tiny example:* $\hat a\lvert 1\rangle=\lvert0\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S3, S4 — *Commonly confused with:* raising vs lowering: dagger raises.
- <a id="raising"></a>**raising operator â†** — *say it as:* "a dagger" — *meaning:* Adds one quantum; $\hat a^\dagger\lvert n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\lvert n+1\rangle$. — *tiny example:* $\hat a^\dagger\lvert 3\rangle=2\lvert4\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S3, S4
- <a id="number-operator"></a>**number operator N̂** — *say it as:* "N hat" — *meaning:* $\hat N=\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ (Eq. 5.11); counts quanta, Hermitian, eigenvalues $0,1,2,\dots$. — *tiny example:* $\hat N\lvert5\rangle=5\lvert5\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S2
- <a id="ket-n"></a>**|n⟩ (number state)** — *say it as:* "ket n" — *meaning:* The common eigenstate of $\hat N$ and $\hat H$ with $n$ quanta (lecture: $|\psi_n\rangle$). — *tiny example:* $\langle2|3\rangle=0$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="aa-commutator"></a>**[â, â†] = 1** — *say it as:* "a a-dagger commutator is one" — *meaning:* The key identity (Eq. 5.10); equivalently $\hat a\hat a^\dagger=\hat N+1$. — *tiny example:* $\hat a\hat a^\dagger\lvert0\rangle=\lvert0\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S1
- <a id="h-ladder"></a>**Ĥ = ħω(N̂ + ½)** — *say it as:* "H equals h-bar omega N plus a half" — *meaning:* The Hamiltonian in ladder form (Eq. 5.16); energies $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)$. — *tiny example:* $E_0=\tfrac12\hbar\omega$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S2
- <a id="zero-point"></a>**zero-point energy** — *say it as:* "zero-point energy" — *meaning:* The ground state energy $\tfrac12\hbar\omega>0$; the oscillator is never at rest. — *tiny example:* $E_0=\tfrac12\hbar\omega$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md); [Day 5](day05.md), Move 5.5 — *Physics view:* confining a particle makes $\Delta p$ large, so kinetic energy of order $\hbar^2/2mL^2$ remains and it can never be at rest ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.4).
- <a id="xp-commutator"></a>**commutator relation [x̂, p̂] = iħ** — *say it as:* "x p commutator is i h-bar" — *meaning:* Position and momentum do not commute; the source of the uncertainty relation. — *tiny example:* $[\hat x,\hat p]\psi=i\hbar\psi$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.5; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md)
- <a id="uncertainty-relation"></a>**uncertainty relation, σₓσ_p ≥ ħ/2** — *say it as:* "sigma x sigma p at least h-bar over two" — *meaning:* The spreads in $x$ and $p$ cannot both be small; a state's product is compared with $\hbar/2$ by computing it yourself (Move 6.3). — *tiny example:* Sharp $p$ forces wide $x$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.3; [Week 4 companion](companions/week04_companion.md), notation decoder — *Commonly confused with:* uncertainty relation (a statement about spreads over many runs) vs measurement disturbance. — *Physics view:* it is the wave statement $\Delta x\,\Delta k\gtrsim\tfrac12$ multiplied by $\hbar$ using $p=\hbar k$ ([P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.4).
- <a id="hermite"></a>**Hermite polynomial Hₙ** — *say it as:* "Hermite polynomial" — *meaning:* The polynomial factor of $\psi_n$ (Eq. 5.31). — *tiny example:* $H_1(z)\propto z$. — *see:* [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="ground-state"></a>**ground state** — *say it as:* "ground state" — *meaning:* The lowest-energy state, $|0\rangle$; annihilated by $\hat a$. — *tiny example:* $E_0=\tfrac12\hbar\omega$. — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.4
- <a id="commutator-algebra"></a>**commutator algebra [A,BC]** — *say it as:* "commutator rule" — *meaning:* $[A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C]$ and $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$: the rules for expanding commutators of products. — *tiny example:* $[\hat N,\hat a]=-\hat a$ (from the second rule). — *see:* [Day 6](day06.md), Move 6.5; [Week 5 companion](companions/week05_companion.md), S3

## Day 7 — Tensor products, entanglement and a mock problem set

Qubit vocabulary and the traps around entanglement.

- <a id="qubit"></a>**qubit** — *say it as:* "qubit" — *meaning:* A two-level quantum system; a spin-½ is one. — *tiny example:* $\alpha\lvert0\rangle+\beta\lvert1\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md); [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md) — *Physics view:* a photon's polarisation is a physical qubit (qubit (physical)), with basis $|H\rangle,|V\rangle$ ([P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1).
- <a id="ket-0-1"></a>**|0⟩, |1⟩** — *say it as:* "ket zero, ket one" — *meaning:* The two basis states of a qubit; in the lecture's arrow language $0\leftrightarrow\uparrow$, $1\leftrightarrow\downarrow$ (labels do not matter, structure does). — *tiny example:* $\lvert01\rangle$: Alice 0, Bob 1. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.1; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="two-qubit-basis"></a>**two-qubit basis |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩** — *say it as:* "ket zero-zero" — *meaning:* Four orthonormal kets; the first label is Alice's, the second Bob's. — *tiny example:* $\lvert01\rangle\neq\lvert10\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.1; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="tensor-product"></a>**tensor product ⊗** — *say it as:* "tensor" — *meaning:* Glues a state of A to a state of B to make a state of the pair. — *tiny example:* $\lvert1\rangle\otimes\lvert0\rangle=\lvert10\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.1; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="kronecker-product"></a>**Kronecker product** — *say it as:* "Kronecker product" — *meaning:* The recipe for computing $\otimes$ on columns and matrices: multiply each entry of the first by the whole second. — *tiny example:* $(1,0)^{\mathsf T}\otimes(a,b)^{\mathsf T}=(a,b,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.1; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), skipped steps 1
- <a id="composite-system"></a>**composite system** — *say it as:* "composite system" — *meaning:* Two systems treated as one; dimensions multiply. — *tiny example:* $3\times4=12$ basis kets. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.1; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="sigma-tensor-1"></a>**σ⊗1 and 1⊗τ** — *say it as:* "sigma tensor identity" — *meaning:* Alice-only and Bob-only operators: act on one slot and leave the other alone. — *tiny example:* $(\sigma_x\otimes\mathbb1)\lvert01\rangle=\lvert11\rangle$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.2; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="product-state"></a>**product state (separable)** — *say it as:* "product state" — *meaning:* A pair-state that can be written (state of A) $\otimes$ (state of B). Test: $a|00\rangle+b|01\rangle+c|10\rangle+d|11\rangle$ is a product iff $ad=bc$. — *tiny example:* $\lvert0\rangle\otimes\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\lvert0\rangle+\lvert1\rangle)$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.3; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), skipped steps 2 — *Commonly confused with:* product vs entangled: two terms in the sum is not enough to be entangled.
- <a id="entangled-state"></a>**entangled state** — *say it as:* "entangled" — *meaning:* Not a product state: $ad\ne bc$. — *tiny example:* $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\lvert00\rangle+\lvert11\rangle)$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.3; [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.5 — *Commonly confused with:* entangled vs product; entangled vs merely correlated-and-communicating. — *Physics view:* at concept level the EPR paradox and Bell inequality ask whether such correlations could come from pre-set answers ([P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2).
- <a id="product-test"></a>**product-state test ad = bc** — *say it as:* "a d equals b c" — *meaning:* The two-qubit test for a product state. — *tiny example:* Amplitudes $1,1,0,0$: $1\cdot0=1\cdot0$, product. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.3
- <a id="partial-measurement"></a>**partial measurement** — *say it as:* "partial measurement" — *meaning:* Measure one qubit: project, renormalise, read off the other qubit's state. — *tiny example:* See Move 7.4. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.4; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), skipped steps 3
- <a id="bell-state"></a>**Bell states Φ±, Ψ±** — *say it as:* "Bell states" — *meaning:* $|\Phi^\pm\rangle=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle)$, $|\Psi^\pm\rangle=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle)$: the orthonormal maximally entangled basis. — *tiny example:* $\Phi^+$ gives agreeing $z$-results. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.5; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="singlet-triplet"></a>**singlet, triplet** — *say it as:* "singlet" — *meaning:* The lecture's names (Eq. 7.6): the singlet is $|\Psi^-\rangle$; the other three Bell states are the triplet. — *tiny example:* $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\uparrow\downarrow-\downarrow\uparrow)$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.5; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="correlation"></a>**correlation** — *say it as:* "correlation" — *meaning:* $\langle AB\rangle-\langle A\rangle\langle B\rangle$; zero for a product state (with $A$ on Alice's slot, $B$ on Bob's). — *tiny example:* $\Phi^+$ with $A=\sigma_z\otimes\mathbb1$, $B=\mathbb1\otimes\sigma_z$: correlation $1$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.5 — *Commonly confused with:* correlated vs communicating: entanglement gives no faster-than-light signalling; the correlation shows only when results are compared. — *Physics view:* agreement between distant results is correlation, not communication: it appears only when the lists are compared ([P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2).
- <a id="tau-sigma"></a>**τ_zσ_z (composite observable)** — *say it as:* "tau z sigma z" — *meaning:* Alice's and Bob's operators multiplied; eigenvalue $+1$ if the $z$ results agree, $-1$ if they disagree. — *tiny example:* $\Psi^+$ has eigenvalue $-1$. — *see:* [Day 7](day07.md), Move 7.5; [Week 7 companion](companions/week07_companion.md), notation decoder
- <a id="superposition"></a>**superposition** — *say it as:* "superposition" — *meaning:* A state built as a weighted sum of others, $\sum\alpha_i|\psi_i\rangle$ (Eq. 2.3). — *tiny example:* $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$. — *see:* [Day 2](day02.md); [Week 2 companion](companions/week02_companion.md) — *Commonly confused with:* superposition vs mixture (a probabilistic blend of separate states; convention, not defined in the course files). — *Physics view:* for waves it is the plain point-by-point addition of displacements that makes interference possible ([P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.2).

## Physics P1 — Energy and Classical State

Classical energy, units and the size of $\hbar$: the physics under $\hat H$. Constants other than $\hbar$ are **(standard value)**; only $\hbar=1.055\times10^{-34}$ J s comes from the lecture. Existing entries [Hamiltonian Ĥ](#hamiltonian), [momentum operator p̂](#momentum-operator) and [ħ (h-bar)](#hbar) carry a "Physics view" sentence pointing here.

- <a id="p1-xva"></a>**x, v, a (position, velocity, acceleration)** — *say it as:* "x, v, a" — *meaning:* Position $x$ in metres, velocity $v=\mathrm dx/\mathrm dt$ in m/s, acceleration $a=\mathrm dv/\mathrm dt$ in m/s². — *tiny example:* A cyclist gaining 2 m/s every second has $a=2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.1
- <a id="p1-momentum"></a>**momentum p = mv** — *say it as:* "p equals m v" — *meaning:* Mass times velocity, in kg m/s; the quantity quantum mechanics uses ($p=\hbar k$), not velocity. — *tiny example:* A 0.2 kg puck at 5 m/s has $p=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m/s}$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.1 — *Commonly confused with:* momentum vs velocity: $p=mv$, so the same $p$ means a different $v$ for a different mass; in $E=p^2/2m$ use $p$, not $v$.
- <a id="p1-classical-state"></a>**classical state** — *say it as:* "classical state" — *meaning:* The minimum information that fixes the future of a particle: position and velocity, equivalently the pair $(x,p)$. — *tiny example:* A 0.1 kg ball at height 2 m rising at 3 m/s has state $(2\ \mathrm m,\ 0.3\ \mathrm{kg\,m/s})$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.1 and Move P1.5
- <a id="p1-determinism"></a>**determinism** — *say it as:* "determinism" — *meaning:* The lecture's word (§1.1) for "the present state fixes the whole future"; the quantum state $\psi(x,t)$ also evolves deterministically, but it gives probabilities for results. — *tiny example:* With no force and $x(0)=0$, $v(0)=1$ m/s, then $x(t)=t$ metres for all later $t$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.1 and Move P1.5 — *Commonly confused with:* deterministic evolution of the state vs a certain measurement outcome: the quantum state evolves deterministically yet outcomes are probabilistic.
- <a id="p1-newton"></a>**Newton's second law, F = ma** — *say it as:* "F equals m a" — *meaning:* The acceleration is force divided by mass (lecture Eq. 1.1); with a force from a potential it becomes a second-order differential equation (Eq. 1.3). — *tiny example:* 3 N on a 1.5 kg trolley gives $a=2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.2
- <a id="p1-force"></a>**force F = −dV/dx** — *say it as:* "force is minus the slope of the potential" — *meaning:* A push or pull in newtons; the lecture (Eq. 1.2) gets it from the potential energy, pointing "downhill". — *tiny example:* $V=5x$ joules gives $F=-5$ N everywhere. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.2
- <a id="p1-potential-energy"></a>**potential energy V(x)** — *say it as:* "V of x" — *meaning:* Stored energy that depends on position, in joules; in the Schrödinger equation it multiplies $\psi$ and no force appears. — *tiny example:* A 2 kg mass 3 m up has $V=mgh=58.86$ J with $g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ (standard value). — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.2 and Move P1.3
- <a id="p1-kinetic-energy"></a>**kinetic energy T = p²/2m** — *say it as:* "p squared over two m" — *meaning:* The energy of motion, $\tfrac12mv^2=p^2/2m$. — *tiny example:* 2 kg at 3 m/s: $T=9$ J (and $p=6$ gives $36/4=9$). — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.3 — *Commonly confused with:* kinetic vs total energy: kinetic energy is only the motion part; total energy adds $V$.
- <a id="p1-total-energy"></a>**total energy (classical Hamiltonian) E = p²/2m + V** — *say it as:* "H of x and p" — *meaning:* Kinetic plus potential energy written in terms of $x$ and $p$; constant in time without friction, and the recipe for the quantum $\hat H$ (see [Hamiltonian Ĥ](#hamiltonian)). — *tiny example:* $V=3x$ joules, $x=2$ m, $p=4\ \mathrm{kg\,m/s}$, $m=1$ kg: $E=8+6=14$ J. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.3 and Move P1.5 — *Commonly confused with:* kinetic vs total energy: $\tfrac12mv^2$ alone is not $E$ unless $V=0$.
- <a id="p1-si-units"></a>**SI units** — *say it as:* "S I units" — *meaning:* Metre, kilogram, second (plus coulomb and tesla) and everything built from them; sanity checks and numbers in Module 3 use them. — *tiny example:* Momentum is kg m s⁻¹. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4
- <a id="p1-joule"></a>**joule, J** — *say it as:* "joule" — *meaning:* The SI unit of energy, $1\ \mathrm J=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}=1\ \mathrm{N\,m}$. — *tiny example:* 1 N pushed over 1 m is 1 J. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4
- <a id="p1-ev"></a>**eV (electron-volt)** — *say it as:* "electron volt" — *meaning:* The atomic-scale energy unit, $1\ \mathrm{eV}=1.602\times10^{-19}$ J (standard value): divide joules by that to get eV. — *tiny example:* $3.204\times10^{-19}$ J is 2.000 eV. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4 — *Commonly confused with:* eV vs J: convert to joules before using $h$, $\hbar$ or $m_e$ in SI formulas, then back to eV to judge the size.
- <a id="p1-dimensional-analysis"></a>**dimensional analysis** — *say it as:* "dimensional analysis" — *meaning:* Check a formula by writing every symbol in base units (kg, m, s) and comparing both sides; it cannot catch a pure factor like $2$ or $\pi$. — *tiny example:* $v=at$: $(\mathrm{m/s^2})(\mathrm s)=\mathrm{m/s}$, passes. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4
- <a id="p1-h"></a>**Planck's constant h** — *say it as:* "h" — *meaning:* $h=2\pi\hbar=6.626\times10^{-34}$ J s (standard value); an action (energy times time). It pairs with frequency $f$ and wavelength $\lambda$, as in $E=hf$ and $p=h/\lambda$. — *tiny example:* Units: J s $=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$. — *see:* [P1](physics/P1.md), Move P1.4; [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4 — *Commonly confused with:* $h$ vs $\hbar$: $\hbar=h/2\pi$ pairs with $\omega$ and $k$; a stray factor of $2\pi$ is the usual symptom of mixing them.

- <a id="p2-wave"></a>**wave** — *say it as:* "wave" — *meaning:* A disturbance whose pattern travels while the medium wiggles in place: a repeating shape in space and a repeating oscillation in time. — *tiny example:* A ripple crossing a pond. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1
- <a id="p2-amplitude"></a>**amplitude A (of a wave)** — *say it as:* "amplitude" — *meaning:* The largest displacement from rest; intensity goes as its square. — *tiny example:* A ripple with crests 0.02 m above rest has $A=0.02$ m. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1 — *Commonly confused with:* wave amplitude (a size, real) vs [probability amplitude](#probability-amplitude) (a complex number whose modulus squared is a probability).
- <a id="p2-wavelength"></a>**wavelength λ** — *say it as:* "lambda" — *meaning:* The distance over which a wave's shape repeats, in metres. — *tiny example:* Ripples 0.5 m apart have $\lambda=0.5$ m. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1 — *Commonly confused with:* wavelength vs wavenumber: $k=2\pi/\lambda$; a long wavelength is a small $k$.
- <a id="p2-frequency"></a>**frequency f** — *say it as:* "f" — *meaning:* Complete oscillations per second at one point, in hertz (s⁻¹). — *tiny example:* 3 ripples per second: $f=3$ Hz. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1 — *Commonly confused with:* frequency vs angular frequency: $\omega=2\pi f$, so 3 Hz is $6\pi\approx18.85\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
- <a id="p2-wavenumber"></a>**wavenumber k** — *say it as:* "k" — *meaning:* $k=2\pi/\lambda$: radians of phase per metre (m⁻¹); in $e^{ikx}$ it sets the momentum, $p=\hbar k$. — *tiny example:* $\lambda=0.5$ m gives $k=4\pi\approx12.57\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1 and Move P2.6 — *Commonly confused with:* wavenumber vs wavelength (inverse-ish, $2\pi$ apart); and $k$ (wavenumber, m⁻¹) vs the spring constant $k$ (N/m) of [P3](physics/P3.md): read the units.
- <a id="p2-angular-frequency"></a>**angular frequency ω** — *say it as:* "omega" — *meaning:* $\omega=2\pi f$: radians of phase per second (s⁻¹); the time phase is $e^{-i\omega t}$ and $E=\hbar\omega$. — *tiny example:* $f=3$ Hz gives $\omega\approx18.85\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1 and Move P2.3; also [ω, ħω](#omega) — *Commonly confused with:* angular frequency vs frequency: cycles per second ($f$) vs radians per second ($\omega$); $\hbar$ goes with $\omega$, $h$ with $f$.
- <a id="p2-wave-speed"></a>**wave speed v = fλ** — *say it as:* "v equals f lambda" — *meaning:* How fast a crest travels; equivalently $v=\omega/k$. — *tiny example:* $f=3$ Hz, $\lambda=0.5$ m: $v=1.5$ m/s. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.1
- <a id="p2-interference-types"></a>**constructive / destructive interference** — *say it as:* "constructive, destructive" — *meaning:* Constructive: contributions with the same sign reinforce (path difference $m\lambda$). Destructive: opposite signs cancel (path difference $(m+\tfrac12)\lambda$). See also [interference](#interference). — *tiny example:* Path difference $2.5\lambda$: dark; $2\lambda$: bright. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.2
- <a id="p2-cross-term"></a>**cross term 2 Re(a₁*a₂)** — *say it as:* "the cross term" — *meaning:* The extra piece in $|a_1+a_2|^2=|a_1|^2+|a_2|^2+2\,\mathrm{Re}(a_1^*a_2)$: it is the interference, and adding probabilities first throws it away. — *tiny example:* Amplitudes 0.2 and 0.2: added-then-squared $0.16$, squared-then-added $0.08$, cross term $0.08$. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.2 — *Commonly confused with:* adding amplitudes then squaring vs squaring then adding.
- <a id="p2-double-slit"></a>**double-slit experiment** — *say it as:* "double slit" — *meaning:* Waves through two narrow slits give bright and dark bands of spacing $\Delta y=\lambda L/d$; single electrons fired one at a time build the same bands from single dots. — *tiny example:* $\lambda=500$ nm, $d=0.25$ mm, $L=1$ m: $\Delta y=2$ mm. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.2 and Move P2.5
- <a id="p2-photon"></a>**photon** — *say it as:* "photon" — *meaning:* A packet of light with energy $hf$ and momentum $h/\lambda$ (standard background: Einstein 1905). — *tiny example:* A 550 nm photon has $E=1240/550\approx2.25$ eV. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-planck"></a>**Planck relation E = hf = ħω** — *say it as:* "E equals h f" — *meaning:* Light of frequency $f$ exchanges energy with matter in lumps of size $hf=\hbar\omega$ (standard background: Planck); the first appearance of $h$. — *tiny example:* $f=5\times10^{14}$ Hz gives $E=3.313\times10^{-19}$ J. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4 — *Commonly confused with:* $hf$ vs $\hbar\omega$: the same number; $h$ with $f$, $\hbar$ with $\omega$.
- <a id="p2-blackbody"></a>**blackbody radiation** — *say it as:* "blackbody radiation" — *meaning:* The glow of a hot object; classical waves-in-a-box reasoning predicted an absurd infinity at short wavelengths, and Planck's lumps $E=hf$ cured it (standard background). — *tiny example:* An iron poker glowing dull red, then orange, then white as it heats. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-photoelectric"></a>**photoelectric effect** — *say it as:* "photoelectric effect" — *meaning:* Electrons leave a metal only if the light's frequency exceeds a threshold, however bright; $K_{\max}=hf-\phi$ (standard background: Einstein). — *tiny example:* Work function 4.0 eV: 250 nm light (4.96 eV) ejects electrons with $K_{\max}=0.96$ eV; 400 nm (3.10 eV) ejects none. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-work-function"></a>**work function φ** — *say it as:* "work function, phi" — *meaning:* The minimum energy needed to free an electron from a metal; the threshold frequency is $f_0=\phi/h$. — *tiny example:* $\phi=4.0$ eV gives a threshold wavelength $1240/4.0=310$ nm. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-photon-momentum"></a>**photon momentum p = h/λ** — *say it as:* "p equals h over lambda" — *meaning:* A photon has $E=pc$, so $p=E/c=h/\lambda$; de Broglie proposed the same relation for matter. — *tiny example:* 620 nm: $p=1.069\times10^{-27}$ kg m/s. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-hc"></a>**hc ≈ 1240 eV nm, and c** — *say it as:* "h c is twelve-forty electron-volt nanometres" — *meaning:* $c=2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s (standard value) and $hc=1.240\times10^{-6}$ eV m, so $E[\mathrm{eV}]=1240/\lambda[\mathrm{nm}]$. — *tiny example:* A 2.0 eV photon has $\lambda=620$ nm. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.4
- <a id="p2-duality"></a>**wave–particle duality** — *say it as:* "wave-particle duality" — *meaning:* One kind of object whose propagation follows a wave-like amplitude while its detection is a single event; not "sometimes a wave, sometimes a particle". — *tiny example:* Electrons fired singly at two slits land as dots that pile up into bands. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.5 — *Commonly confused with:* duality as "wave now, particle later": the wave-like part is the amplitude, the particle-like part is the detection.
- <a id="p2-spectral-line"></a>**spectral line** — *say it as:* "spectral line" — *meaning:* Light emitted at one sharp wavelength when an atom drops between two energy levels, $hf=E_{\text{upper}}-E_{\text{lower}}$; the lecture (W3 §3.1) cites lines as evidence for discrete energies, and the photon-emission step is standard physics. — *tiny example:* A 3.10 eV gap emits $1240/3.10=400$ nm. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.6
- <a id="p2-fringe-spacing"></a>**fringe spacing Δy = λL/d** — *say it as:* "delta y equals lambda L over d" — *meaning:* The spacing of bright bands from two slits a distance $d$ apart, screen at distance $L\gg d$ (standard small-angle result). — *tiny example:* Doubling $d$ halves $\Delta y$. — *see:* [P2](physics/P2.md), Move P2.2

## Physics P3 — Springs, Standing Waves and Confinement

Why confined waves have discrete energies and why "confined" means "not at rest". Existing entries [zero-point energy](#zero-point), [energy level, Eₙ](#energy-level), [quantised](#quantised), [uncertainty relation, σₓσ_p ≥ ħ/2](#uncertainty-relation) and [harmonic oscillator](#harmonic-oscillator) carry a "Physics view" sentence pointing here.

- <a id="p3-shm"></a>**simple harmonic motion** — *say it as:* "simple harmonic motion" — *meaning:* Motion under $F=-kx$: $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\varphi)$ with $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$; energy swaps between kinetic and potential with total $\tfrac12kA^2$. — *tiny example:* A 2 kg mass on a 50 N/m spring: $\omega=5$ rad/s. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.1
- <a id="p3-spring-constant"></a>**spring constant k** — *say it as:* "spring constant, k" — *meaning:* The stiffness in $F=-kx$ (N/m); it gives the lecture's $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$ (§5.1). — *tiny example:* 0.04 m stretch of a 50 N/m spring: $F=2$ N. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.1 — *Commonly confused with:* spring constant $k$ (N/m) vs wavenumber $k$ (m⁻¹): different quantities with the same letter; check units.
- <a id="p3-period"></a>**period T** — *say it as:* "period" — *meaning:* Time for one full cycle, $T=2\pi/\omega=1/f$. — *tiny example:* $\omega=5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$: $T=1.26$ s. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.1
- <a id="p3-turning-point"></a>**turning point** — *say it as:* "turning point" — *meaning:* A place $x=\pm A$ where the mass stops for an instant and reverses; all its energy is potential there, $E=\tfrac12kx^2$. — *tiny example:* $E=0.25$ J, $k=50$ N/m: turning at $x=\pm0.1$ m. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.1
- <a id="p3-classical-density"></a>**classical probability density (mass on a spring)** — *say it as:* "classical density" — *meaning:* The fraction of time spent near $x$, proportional to $1/|v|$: high near the turning points, low at the centre, zero beyond them. — *tiny example:* A pendulum bob is most likely caught near the ends of its swing. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.2 — *Commonly confused with:* this classical shape vs a quantum $|\psi|^2$, which can be non-zero beyond the turning points.
- <a id="p3-standing-wave"></a>**standing wave** — *say it as:* "standing wave" — *meaning:* A wave pattern that stays in place, with fixed nodes; on a length $L$ pinned at both ends $\lambda_n=2L/n$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$. — *tiny example:* A 1.2 m string vibrating in its $n=3$ mode has $\lambda=0.8$ m. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3
- <a id="p3-node"></a>**node** — *say it as:* "node" — *meaning:* A point of a standing wave that never moves; the pinned ends are nodes. — *tiny example:* The $n=2$ string mode has a node in the middle. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3
- <a id="p3-confinement"></a>**confinement** — *say it as:* "confinement" — *meaning:* Restricting a wave or particle to a region, so only waves that fit the boundaries are allowed. — *tiny example:* A wavelength of $1.3L$ does not fit a string pinned at both ends. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3
- <a id="p3-quantisation"></a>**quantisation (physical)** — *say it as:* "quantisation" — *meaning:* Only certain values allowed, arising because confinement lets only some standing waves fit; with de Broglie's $p=h/\lambda$ this gives $E_n=n^2h^2/8mL^2=n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/2mL^2$. Compare [quantised](#quantised) (the boundary-condition view). — *tiny example:* If $E_1=0.5$ eV in a box then $E_2=2.0$ eV and $E_3=4.5$ eV. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.3 — *Commonly confused with:* the box starts at $n=1$ but the oscillator ladder at $n=0$.
- <a id="p3-uncertainty-principle"></a>**uncertainty principle (Δx Δk form), Heisenberg's relation** — *say it as:* "delta x delta k at least a half" — *meaning:* A wave confined to a width $\Delta x$ needs a spread of wavenumbers $\Delta k\gtrsim1/(2\Delta x)$; with $p=\hbar k$ this is $\Delta x\,\Delta p\gtrsim\hbar/2$, the same as [Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2](#delta-x-delta-p). — *tiny example:* $\Delta x=2$ nm gives $\Delta k\gtrsim2.5\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $\Delta p\gtrsim2.6\times10^{-26}$ kg m/s. — *see:* [P3](physics/P3.md), Move P3.4 — *Commonly confused with:* the relation about spreads over many runs vs a disturbance caused by measuring.

## Physics P4 — Magnetism and Spin

The physics under the Stern-Gerlach experiment. Silver-atom and precession background is **(standard background)** unless marked; $\mu_B$, $e$, $m_e$ and $g$ are **(standard value)**. Existing entries [Stern-Gerlach experiment](#stern-gerlach), [inhomogeneous field](#inhomogeneous), [magnetic moment μ](#magnetic-moment), [Ŝ_z, Ŝₓ and the ħ/2 convention](#spin-operators) and [σₓ, σ_y, σ_z (Pauli matrices)](#pauli-matrices) carry a "Physics view" sentence pointing here.

- <a id="p4-tesla"></a>**magnetic field B, tesla (T)** — *say it as:* "B", "tesla" — *meaning:* The thing magnets push against; measured in tesla, $1\ \mathrm T=1\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-2}A^{-1}}$. — *tiny example:* A fridge magnet's field is of order a few millitesla. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1
- <a id="p4-torque"></a>**torque τ = μ × B** — *say it as:* "tau equals mu cross B" — *meaning:* The twist a field exerts on a moment, $|\tau|=\mu B\sin\theta$; it turns the moment but does not push its centre of mass. — *tiny example:* $\mu=2\times10^{-23}$ J/T, $B=0.2$ T at $\theta=90^\circ$: $\tau=4\times10^{-24}$ N m. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1 — *Commonly confused with:* torque $\tau$ here vs $\tau$ as Bob's operator in [τ_zσ_z](#tau-sigma); torque vs force: a torque alone gives no net push.
- <a id="p4-dipole-energy"></a>**dipole energy U = −μ·B** — *say it as:* "U equals minus mu dot B" — *meaning:* The energy of a moment in a field, $-\mu B\cos\theta$: lowest when aligned, highest when anti-aligned. — *tiny example:* $\mu=2\times10^{-23}$ J/T, $B=0.2$ T, aligned: $U=-4\times10^{-24}$ J. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1
- <a id="p4-gradient"></a>**field gradient dB_z/dz and the force F_z = μ_z dB_z/dz** — *say it as:* "d B z by d z" — *meaning:* The force on a dipole is $-\mathrm dU/\mathrm dz=\mu_z\,\mathrm dB_z/\mathrm dz$ (lecture §6.1); a uniform field has zero gradient, so a twist but no net force. — *tiny example:* $\mu_z=10^{-23}$ J/T, gradient 40 T/m: $F=4\times10^{-22}$ N. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.2 — *Commonly confused with:* force vs field gradient: the strength of $B$ does not matter, its slope does.
- <a id="p4-angular-momentum"></a>**angular momentum (orbital) L = r × p** — *say it as:* "L equals r cross p" — *meaning:* The amount of turning of a mass about a centre; $|L|=mvr$ on a circle, in J s like $\hbar$. — *tiny example:* 0.4 kg at 2 m/s on a circle of radius 0.5 m: $L=0.4$ kg m²/s. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.3 — *Commonly confused with:* spin vs orbital angular momentum: orbital comes from motion around a centre, spin is intrinsic to the particle.
- <a id="p4-spin"></a>**spin (intrinsic angular momentum)** — *say it as:* "spin" — *meaning:* Angular momentum a particle has by nature, not because a part of it circles a centre; the lecture warns the little-arrow picture will deceive you. — *tiny example:* An electron has $S_z=\pm\hbar/2=\pm5.275\times10^{-35}$ J s (convention). — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.4; see also [spin-up/spin-down](#spin-up-down) — *Commonly confused with:* spin vs orbital angular momentum, and spin vs a spinning ball, which could point at any angle.
- <a id="p4-quantised-spin"></a>**quantised spin** — *say it as:* "quantised spin" — *meaning:* $S_z$ takes only two values for spin one-half, seen as two spots on the screen rather than a smear. — *tiny example:* A beam of unpaired-electron atoms splits into two beams, nothing in between. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.3 and Move P4.4 — *Commonly confused with:* the classical prediction (a continuous smear for any orientation) vs the observed two spots.
- <a id="p4-bohr-magneton"></a>**Bohr magneton μ_B** — *say it as:* "Bohr magneton" — *meaning:* The natural unit of magnetic moment, $\mu_B=e\hbar/2m_e=9.274\times10^{-24}$ J/T (standard value). — *tiny example:* In $B=2$ T, $\mu_BB=1.855\times10^{-23}$ J. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1 and Move P4.4
- <a id="p4-g-factor"></a>**g-factor (and precession)** — *say it as:* "g factor" — *meaning:* The number in $\boldsymbol\mu=\tfrac{gq}{2m}\mathbf S$ (lecture); $g=2$ for the electron (standard value). Standard background: a moment with angular momentum circles, or precesses, around the field rather than lining up. — *tiny example:* A tilted spinning top precesses around the vertical. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1 and Move P4.4
- <a id="p4-beam-splitting"></a>**beam splitting** — *say it as:* "beam splitting" — *meaning:* A magnet separating a beam into one beam per outcome; a second magnet can recombine them. — *tiny example:* A $z$-magnet gives an upper and a lower beam. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.4 and Move P4.5
- <a id="p4-sequential"></a>**sequential measurement (magnet chain)** — *say it as:* "sequential measurement" — *meaning:* Beams fed from one magnet into the next: the same axis twice repeats; perpendicular axes give 50/50 (lecture Eq. 6.2), and for axes at angle $\theta$ the probabilities are $(1\pm\cos\theta)/2$ (lecture Eqs. 6.3 and 6.4); recombining unobserved beams shows interference. See also [sequential-measurement paradox](#sequential). — *tiny example:* Same axis twice: the beam stays in the same output. — *see:* [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.5

## Physics P5 — Measurement, Interpretations and Quantum Technology

Photon polarisation as a qubit, the concept-level EPR/Bell story, neutral names for the Week 3 §3.4 positions, and the physical-reasoning kit. EPR, Bell, sifting and silver-atom background are **(standard background)**; many-worlds and decoherence are not in the lecture section and are not described here. Existing entries [qubit](#qubit), [entangled state](#entangled-state) and [correlation](#correlation) carry a "Physics view" sentence pointing here.

- <a id="p5-polarisation"></a>**polarisation** — *say it as:* "polarisation" — *meaning:* The line along which the electric field of light wobbles; light wobbling along one line is linearly polarised. — *tiny example:* Sunlight glare off a lake is mostly horizontally polarised. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1
- <a id="p5-polariser"></a>**polariser** — *say it as:* "polariser" — *meaning:* A sheet with a preferred axis that passes the part of the field along the axis and blocks the part at right angles; after a photon passes it is in the axis state. — *tiny example:* A polariser at $45^\circ$ to vertical light passes half. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1
- <a id="p5-intensity"></a>**I, I₀ (intensity)** — *say it as:* "I, I naught" — *meaning:* Power per area in W/m²; $I_0$ is the intensity before a polariser. — *tiny example:* $I_0=8\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ through a polariser at $45^\circ$ gives $I=4\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1
- <a id="p5-theta"></a>**θ (angle)** — *say it as:* "theta" — *meaning:* An angle between two directions: polariser axis vs light polarisation (P5), or moment vs field (P4). — *tiny example:* $\theta=20^\circ$ gives $\cos^2\theta=0.883$. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1; [P4](physics/P4.md), Move P4.1 — *Commonly confused with:* polarisation angle vs spin angle: a photon state at angle $\theta$ has amplitudes $\cos\theta,\sin\theta$, a spin-½ state pointing at $\theta$ has $\cos(\theta/2),\sin(\theta/2)$.
- <a id="p5-malus"></a>**Malus's law I = I₀ cos²θ** — *say it as:* "Malus's law" — *meaning:* Transmitted intensity through a polariser at angle $\theta$; it is the Born rule $|\text{amplitude}|^2$ seen from the beam instead of the single photon. — *tiny example:* $I_0=50\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$, $\theta=60^\circ$: $I=12.5\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1
- <a id="p5-photon-state"></a>**photon polarisation state |α⟩ = cos α|H⟩ + sin α|V⟩** — *say it as:* "ket alpha" — *meaning:* A photon polarised at angle $\alpha$ from horizontal, in the orthonormal basis $|H\rangle,|V\rangle$; passing a polariser at $\theta$ has amplitude $\cos(\alpha-\theta)$ and probability $\cos^2(\alpha-\theta)$. — *tiny example:* $\alpha-\theta=20^\circ$: pass probability 0.883. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.1 and Move P5.2
- <a id="p5-epr"></a>**EPR paradox** — *say it as:* "E P R paradox" — *meaning:* Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen's argument that if distant results always agree, each particle must have carried its answer, so quantum mechanics is incomplete (standard background). — *tiny example:* Sealed envelopes with matching cards are the classical picture. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2
- <a id="p5-hidden-variables"></a>**hidden variables (local)** — *say it as:* "hidden variables" — *meaning:* Extra pre-set information beyond the wave function that would fix each result; the lecture (§3.4) names them in the realist position. "Local" means nothing done to one particle changes the other instantly (standard background). — *tiny example:* The matching cards in the envelope picture. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2 and Move P5.3
- <a id="p5-bell"></a>**Bell inequality (concept)** — *say it as:* "Bell inequality" — *meaning:* Standard background (not in the lecture): statistics at several alignments obey an inequality if local hidden variables are true; quantum mechanics predicts a violation for entangled pairs, and experiments favour quantum mechanics. — *tiny example:* Perfect agreement at one alignment cannot decide it; several alignments can. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2
- <a id="p5-correlated-not-communicating"></a>**correlated vs communicating** — *say it as:* "correlated, not communicating" — *meaning:* Each side's own results look like fair coin tosses; agreement shows only when the two lists are compared over an ordinary channel, so entanglement sends no message. See also [correlation](#correlation). — *tiny example:* Two matching lists of random bits reveal nothing until a phone call compares them. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.2 — *Commonly confused with:* correlated vs communicating: agreement is not a signal.
- <a id="p5-realist"></a>**realist position** — *say it as:* "realist" — *meaning:* The lecture's first §3.4 answer: the particle was at the measured place before, and the wave function is incomplete so a hidden variable is needed; Einstein is named. — *tiny example:* "It was at C all along." — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.3 — *Commonly confused with:* interpretation vs prediction: none of the three positions changes the outcome probabilities computed in this module.
- <a id="p5-copenhagen"></a>**orthodox (Copenhagen interpretation)** — *say it as:* "orthodox, Copenhagen" — *meaning:* The lecture's second §3.4 answer: the particle was not definitely anywhere until the measurement produced a definite value; associated with Bohr and the most widely accepted view, though "the act of measurement" remains puzzling. — *tiny example:* "There was no definite position before I looked." — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.3 — *Commonly confused with:* interpretation vs prediction: it shares its predictions with the other positions.
- <a id="p5-agnostic"></a>**agnostic position** — *say it as:* "agnostic" — *meaning:* The lecture's third §3.4 answer: decline to answer, since no measurement can test a statement about the state before a measurement; the lecture quotes Pauli. — *tiny example:* "That question has no testable content." — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.3 — *Commonly confused with:* interpretation vs prediction: choosing a position does not change any calculated probability.
- <a id="p5-no-cloning"></a>**no-cloning theorem** — *say it as:* "no cloning" — *meaning:* An unknown quantum state cannot be copied, so eavesdropping disturbs the state and can be detected (stated in the lecture §7.4, cited from outside sources). — *tiny example:* Eve cannot make a perfect copy of a photon to keep. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.4
- <a id="p5-key-distribution"></a>**key distribution** — *say it as:* "key distribution" — *meaning:* Getting a long random bit string, the secret key, from Alice to Bob; Ekert's 1992 scheme (lecture §7.4) uses polarisation-entangled photon pairs and identical random results at aligned polarisers. — *tiny example:* Aligned results $+1,-1,-1,+1$ relabel to bits $1,0,0,1$. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.4
- <a id="p5-eavesdropper"></a>**eavesdropper (Eve)** — *say it as:* "eavesdropper, Eve" — *meaning:* A spy on the line; a smart Eve who knows Bob's direction could insert a polariser along it, so Alice and Bob choose orientations at random and check the other runs. — *tiny example:* Eve measuring along another direction makes the correlations differ from those expected. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.4
- <a id="p5-sifted-key"></a>**sifted key (sifting)** — *say it as:* "sifted key" — *meaning:* The bits kept after Alice and Bob publicly compare which setting each used and keep only matching rounds; the word is standard terminology, not the lecture's, and the two-setting picture is a teaching model. — *tiny example:* 400 rounds with matching probability $\tfrac12$: about 200 bits survive. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.4
- <a id="p5-sensing"></a>**quantum sensing** — *say it as:* "quantum sensing" — *meaning:* Using a two-level quantum system as a meter: a spin's level splitting $2\mu B$ gives a response frequency $f=\Delta E/h$ that reads $B$ (standard background). — *tiny example:* $\mu=\mu_B$, $B=2.0$ mT: $\Delta E=3.71\times10^{-26}$ J, $f\approx56$ MHz. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.4
- <a id="p5-order-of-magnitude"></a>**order of magnitude** — *say it as:* "order of magnitude" — *meaning:* The nearest power of ten; round to one significant figure and ask whether the size is plausible. — *tiny example:* A 1 kg mass moving 1 m/s over 1 m has action about $10^{34}\hbar$. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.5
- <a id="p5-limiting-case"></a>**limiting case** — *say it as:* "limiting case" — *meaning:* Push a parameter to 0 or $\infty$ where you already know the answer and check the formula reduces to it. — *tiny example:* $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\to0$ as $m\to\infty$: an infinitely heavy mass barely oscillates. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.5
- <a id="p5-symmetry-check"></a>**symmetry check** — *say it as:* "symmetry check" — *meaning:* Ask what must stay unchanged if you relabel or swap something, and test your result against it. — *tiny example:* $(|00\rangle+3|01\rangle+3|10\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt{20}$ is unchanged when the two qubits are swapped. — *see:* [P5](physics/P5.md), Move P5.5
