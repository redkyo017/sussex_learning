Throughout this module, you will learn about the history of quantum mechanics, from its early beginnings with the work of Max Planck and Albert Einstein to the development of the Copenhagen interpretation. At the heart of quantum mechanics is the concept of a ‘quantum’, which is the smallest unit of energy that exists. One of the most intriguing aspects of quantum mechanics is the concept of ‘superposition’, which describes the state of a quantum particle that is in two or more states simultaneously. This means that a particle can exist in multiple places at once or be in multiple energy states simultaneously. This is a strange concept to grasp, but it has been experimentally verified numerous times and has significant implications for our understanding of the universe.

Another fascinating feature of quantum mechanics is ‘entanglement’ which occurs when two particles become connected in such a way that the state of one particle affects the state of the other, no matter how far apart they are. This has been demonstrated experimentally and has led to the development of technologies such as quantum teleportation and quantum computing. You will also learn about the mathematics of quantum mechanics, including the Schrödinger equation, which describes the behaviour of quantum particles. In addition to the theory, this module will also introduce you to some of the practical applications of quantum mechanics. You will learn about quantum cryptography, which is used to secure communications.

As you progress through this module, you will gain a deeper understanding of the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics and how they have revolutionised our understanding of the natural world. You will also develop critical thinking and problem-solving skills that are essential for a career in physics and many other STEM fields.

So, get ready to embark on a journey into the fascinating world of quantum mechanics. The mysteries of the quantum world are waiting to be unravelled, and this module is your first step in that direction.

### 2. What you will learn and how

The goal of this module is to improve quantum literacy and familiarise students with the fundamental concepts and mathematical frameworks of modern quantum science. It will give students an overarching introduction to quantum physics and present them with the tools needed to understand the practical implementations of quantum technologies. The topics covered highlight the foundational phenomena at the forefront of the second quantum revolution on which the field of emerging quantum technologies is built.

Over seven weeks we will cover the following topics:

Language and tools of quantum mechanics
The wave functions, operators and observables
Probabilistic nature of the measurement in Quantum Mechanics
Schrödinger equation
Quantum harmonic oscillator
Stern-Gerlach experiment
Quantum entanglement and composite systems
One of the most effective ways to apply and consolidate your learning of quantum physics is through solving problems. Each week you will be asked to solve two sets of problems.

Task problems: these are your opportunities to practise the concepts that are introduced each week in the Individual task. You will share your solutions to these problems with others on the module via the weekly discussion board to receive peer and/or tutor feedback. The weekly discussion board can also be used to ask questions and to give and receive support within the group.
Portfolio problems: having had an opportunity to work collaboratively on solving problems with your peers, additional problems are provided each week for your Independent study. These form part of your Assessment 2: Portfolio. You are encouraged to develop these problems throughout the module independently as part of your portfolio assessment.
At the end of each week you will have a short quiz to test your understanding.

Live sessions will provide you the opportunity to interact with the academic team, ask questions, or clarify points you covered during learning. For more information about the sessions, please check the module calendar and the Live sessions pages across the module.

The learning journey of quantum physics can be both challenging and rewarding, as the subject requires an understanding of mathematical concepts and their application to physical systems. However, the effort is worth it, as you can gain a deep understanding of the theories and mathematical tools used in quantum mechanics, and develop a strong foundation for further study in the field.

### 3. Module learning outcomes
By the end of this module you will be able to:

demonstrate a systematic understanding of the basic concepts and postulates of quantum physics (for example, wave functions, operators, uncertainty principles and expectation values)
analyse composite quantum systems and entanglement
understand and assess the principles on which quantum technologies operate
master quantum mechanical principles and algebraic approaches and deploy them to assess real-world technologies, such as quantum sensing.

#### Assessment submission guidelines
Assessment submission
It is your responsibility to submit your assessment, in the right format and in the right place. All assessments have been set up for electronic submission and have a submission point under the ‘Assignments’ tab of this module. You will be able to use this point to submit work while it is open for submission and review feedback once it has been released by your tutor.

If you are having difficulty submitting your assessment, please refer to the e-Submission guidance for students  or contact your Student Success Advisor.

Anonymity
The marking of assessed work should be conducted anonymously by candidate numbers rather than names as far as reasonably practicable (for some types of assessments, anonymity is impossible, such as presentations).

When submitting your work, please use your candidate number. Do not include your name in the title of the document, in the file name, or within it.

If you are having difficulty submitting your work, please do not email your work to your Module Leader or Online Tutor. Instead, please contact your Student Success Advisor.

Late submission
If you miss an assessment submission deadline, you can submit the assessment within 24 hours of the published deadline, provided your assessment allows it. If you submit up to 24 hours later than the assessment deadline, your assessment will incur a penalty deduction of five percentage points (not 5% of the actual mark).

For example, if you’re awarded a mark of 70% for an assessment, but submit within the 24-hour lateness period, your overall mark will drop to 65%.

No work shall be accepted after the 24-hour penalty period: it will be classed as a non-submission and a mark of zero will be recorded against the assessment.

Late submission is available for specific assessments.

If you’re unsure whether late submission applies to a particular assessment on a module, please check Sussex Direct  and navigate to Study > Timetable > Assessment Deadlines.

This will list all your module assessment types and deadlines. If the assessment has a 24-hour penalty period, you will see (+1) next to the deadline date and time. If it does not have this, the assessment does not have a late submission period. Alternatively, you can contact your Student Success Advisor.

If you’re dealing with difficult circumstances, you must notify your Student Success Advisor promptly. They will direct you to the appropriate university service and/or guidance.
